# Generate reviewed NL-to-SOP examples

Before designing targets or data, read `docs/wire_types.html` and check syntax against the current executable grammar, not the proposed keyword-only syntax without parentheses or commas. In the current grammar, `@id type` declares a wire, `$id` consumes a value, `?x` is a local logical unknown, and `~id` references an approved definition. The proposed `?other` for context injection/metaprogramming and `~other` for named internal handles do not change these current meanings and are not generation targets; do not invent other conventions. Until the new syntax is explicitly approved, do not generate data in that syntax or start training.

**Quoted text in generated targets:** Follow [DS004](../../docs/specs/DS004-sop.md) and the [shared quoted-text syntax](../../docs/wire_typs/syntax.html#quoted-text). Preserve the natural spelling and capitalization of proper names; capitalization alone does not establish a canonical entity ID. In fields that consume the rest of a line, bare text may be accepted as input, but canonical generated and training free-text literals must always be JSON-style double-quoted, even a single-word name. A token-list field requires quotes for a multiword string to remain one argument. Escape embedded `"` and `\` using a JSON string encoder, not single or smart quote delimiters. For JSONL, JSON-encode the *entire* SOP text after quoting the SOP literal: the outer JSON layer escapes the inner quotes and backslashes again. Check exact quotation provenance against the value after decoding both layers, not against visibly escaped JSONL bytes. `$ref`, `?var`, `~handle`, and enumerated values such as `source user` and `language en` are not free text to quote. A quoted mention is not an identity lookup; use `resolve` against the reviewed lexicon and consume its `$ref` rather than silently resolving a name. Quoting a sigil or keyword keeps it as text in text fields, but do not assume this holds universally in atom arguments: the current atom parser can lose the string/variable distinction for variable-shaped strings such as `"?x"`.

The immediate priority is to validate that the **small formalizer model** reliably produces simple SOP from natural language; evaluate this task separately from ChatSOP as a complete system and from the coding-agent workflow for documents and knowledge. `jsEval` does not belong in the small formalizer's targets, new examples, or training. Complex expressions hidden in `value.data` are not an acceptable workaround either: use only simple constructs from the approved formalizer profile, or ask for clarification if the request cannot be represented. The current `jsEval` is a restricted expression interpreter, not full JavaScript; future full-JavaScript support is unspecified.

**Problem formulation is not code generation:** Teach declarative AND, inclusive OR, explicit nested grouping, comparisons, and numeric constraints as part of the small formalizer's task. Executable `all`/`any`/`end` blocks now apply to `query.where/filter` and `constraint.require/claim`; use these blocks for canonical Boolean combinations rather than parenthesized Boolean formulas. Atom punctuation and arithmetic leaves retain their current grammar. Preserve the condition tree and correlated assignments in independent semantic oracles; do not flatten alternatives into conjunctions. Selected/filter variables must be guaranteed across alternatives or by a shared conjunct. Simple introductory lessons do not cap expressiveness: excluding `jsEval` does not exclude `constraint`. The model states the problem; the system solves it. Formalizer is an NL-to-SOP role, not a model size; a coding agent can perform the role under its own host-approved contract. Explicit CNL remains required by the current conversational circuit contract, but host-supplied rendering is the intended simpler model interface, not yet an executable omission rule.

The existing corpus may contain `jsEval` and other targets outside this boundary: mark it **pending migration/review**, not an approved small-model curriculum, and do not regenerate it now. Agree on the syntax and target profile first; only then review the data and evaluate the small model. This exclusion does not prohibit host-authorized, controlled computation by the coding agent: approved reasoning programs may be extended when needed (algorithms, graph search, collection/graph construction), with contracts, permissions, and verification, without automatically turning them into small-model targets.

After the syntax and small-model profile are approved, read `docs/legacy/requirements/07-date.md` before building new executable SOP worlds and targets. Use the prompts in `server/llm.mjs`, not a parallel version. Every example has a profile, group, and split. Do not move paraphrases between splits.

Generate linguistic variations with a teacher from a fixed target. Check time, negation, question scope, and identity; a target is not approved merely because it parses. Include appropriate clarifications, not just answerable questions.

Keep user assertions supplied as question context separate from persistent knowledge extracted from documents and approved with a source. A contextual assertion does not automatically become a published fact or ground truth; mark unknown premises and verify every quote, provenance, negation, and time interval before approving a target.

Only after agreement on the syntax and profile, run `tools/check-data.mjs --execute` for new or revised data. Maintain a human holdout and distinct logical compositions. Document the teacher model, parameters, sources, and review decisions. Do not call programmatic examples “validated natural Romanian” without review.

For the v3 profile, separate from the formalizer's minimal curriculum, also read `docs/legacy/requirements/27-date-si-invatare-v3.md`. The type catalog is `sop/contracts/wires.json`; the existence of types and examples involving abduction/planning does not make them small-model targets or require reducing them to factual questions.
