## Normative verified-current extraction

### Authoring, identity, and recording

1. A model-origin circuit MUST contain only `premise`, `query`, and `constraint` declarations. The approved host is responsible for compiling and authorizing `resolve`, `pack`, `solve`, `cnl`, `remember`, and, if required, `clarify`; trusted operations being parseable does not enlarge the model's permission (`docs/specs/DS004-sop.md:14-22`; `docs/specs/DS006-reasoning.md:38`). Atom arguments MUST be whitespace-separated, one to four terms, with optional explicit `not`; query-local variables do not link unrelated queries (`docs/specs/DS004-sop.md:26-28,39-47`). JSON quotes cannot make variable-shaped atom text a ground constant (`docs/specs/DS004-sop.md:47`).
2. A `premise` is conditional interpreted context with `holds`, optionally `valid` (default `timeless`), not a sourced `fact`, documentary citation or implied write. Its lowered origin is `model-interpretation`; the host, not the model declaration, supplies original input provenance. The host can pass premises into `solve.assume`, retaining hypothetical derivations; `remember` rejects them (`sop/lower.mjs:9-12`; `docs/specs/DS004-sop.md:16,20`; `reasoning/reasoner.mjs:101-116`). A trusted `fact` value is itself not recorded: recording requires a separately authorized `remember`, and repository commit is a distinct step (`docs/specs/DS004-sop.md:71-75`; `docs/specs/DS010-common-contracts.md:17,20`).
3. Host orchestration MUST generate clarification for unresolved identity or a required downstream scalar that is missing/nonunique. In particular `constraint.select` requests a checked scalar, and ambiguity requires clarification even when the constraint claim is entailed. The clarification packet carries `pendingSop`, `required`, and `next`; mere absence of evidence remains `unknown` rather than asking for user clarification (`sop/declarative.mjs:36-49,89-95,123-139`; `docs/specs/DS004-sop.md:18,22`; `docs/specs/DS010-common-contracts.md:31,37`).

### Linking, outputs and solver semantics

4. A linker MUST match a query's canonical predicate, arity and polarity against approved rule heads, standardize rule variables apart, propagate compatible constants into rule-body subgoals, and retrieve both requested and opposing evidence under view/time/budget limits. `linkPlan` records goals, producer steps and actual retrievals; a candidate is not an inference merely because retrieval found it (`reasoning/linker.mjs:11-39,41-58`). The inference engine, rather than the linker or lexical similarity, performs joins and closure (`reasoning/linker.mjs:1-3`; `reasoning/reasoner.mjs:10-18`).
5. `output ?x one` reserves a future producer-owned wire; it MUST NOT collide with an explicitly declared wire or another producer. `?x` remains local until a checked output materializes consumable `$x`. `one` binds only one distinct value after completed search and without identified conflict; `many` preserves distinct values; `rows` keeps correlated assignments together; `count` requires a count query; `status` may expose the unresolved formal result. Incompleteness, conflict, ambiguity and no answer cannot be replaced with an arbitrary first scalar (`sop/outputs.mjs:1-23,25-51`; `docs/specs/DS004-sop.md:14,28-30`). For numeric projections the finite backend considers all base models, while Z3 explicitly checks that no different selected value satisfies the premises; `many`/`rows` Z3 projections are unsupported (`reasoning/backends/constraints.mjs:5-12,18-33`).
6. The function-free Horn kernel joins complete rule bodies, intersects valid-time intervals and carries used premises into proof. Explicit contrary evidence may yield `refuted` or `both`; absence is not negation and conflicting evidence does not entail an arbitrary proposition. A cutoff stays `complete:false`, including before claims of uniqueness or count (`reasoning/reasoner.mjs:1-3,10-18,63-94`; `docs/specs/DS006-reasoning.md:16-18`). A required theory or numeric constraint MUST NOT be silently ignored on an incompatible route (`reasoning/registry.mjs:29-33`).
7. `reference` uses bounded JS for its declared modes. `advanced` MAY invoke SWI for suitable point-in-time Horn work and Z3 for suitable constraints or optimization if available; otherwise the actual JS route/fallback MUST be disclosed. SWI closure is checked against JS and JS reconstructs the proof, so its proof is not independent. A satisfying numeric model alone is not entailment, uniqueness, or optimality (`reasoning/registry.mjs:14-17,34-52`; `reasoning/backends/horn.mjs:5-17`; `reasoning/backends/constraints.mjs:14-35`; `reasoning/optimization.mjs:15-28`).

### Retrieval, exploration and epistemic packets

8. A retrieval strategy MUST respect the host-selected repository/session, query time/view and budgets; the linker consumes its admitted rows and exposes `probes`, `coverage`, `complete`, and selected strategy. Completeness denotes the explored retained view, not all past knowledge or the outside world; unverified retrieval and hypotheses MUST NOT become ordinary proof premises (`reasoning/linker.mjs:41-58`; `docs/specs/DS006-reasoning.md:16-18`). Reasoning-strategy registration is orthogonal to memory selection and validates only the minimal result shape (`reasoning/registry.mjs:1-3,21-25`; `docs/specs/DS006-reasoning.md:12-14`).
9. Bounded abduction MAY generate ground missing-premise hypotheses, but excludes the observed target as its own explanation and returns subset-minimal conditional explanations without recording them. Diagnosis ranks supplied ground tests by separation of explanation pairs and does not perform real-world tests. Association ranks explicitly supplied traces as hints; induction counts support, counterexamples and unknowns on supplied cases (a missing consequent counts as a counterexample only when the supplied trace is marked `closed`); analogy proposes mapped hypotheses; a bounded plan is not an executed action. Simulation recalculates an isolated hypothetical state and requires an explicit causal rule module for counterfactual mode (`reasoning/abduction.mjs:12-46`; `reasoning/learning.mjs:13-27,29-64`; `reasoning/worlds.mjs:8-43`). None of these proposals becomes an approved rule or observed fact by scoring (`reasoning/learning.mjs:43-52`; `docs/specs/DS006-reasoning.md:16-18`).
10. The runtime result, its output-port binding and the epistemic projection are separate objects/decisions. `epistemicResult` maps a reported runtime `status` but MUST retain `runtime_status`, `complete`, `hypothetical` and `epistemic`; conflict takes precedence over hypothetical positive support, and hypothetical support projects to `PLAUSIBLE` instead of unconditional `ENTAILED`. Missing completeness is `null`, not a complete search; `stored` denotes an operational receipt, not truth (`eval/contracts.mjs:3-27`; `docs/specs/DS010-common-contracts.md:16,26-48`).

### Operational evidence boundary

11. Evaluation records MUST distinguish model-formalizer rows from trusted system circuits with `evaluation_track`; fixed suite/config hashes and supplied model manifests are experiment identities, not verification of model weights or language performance (`docs/specs/DS010-common-contracts.md:15,18`; `docs/specs/DS006-reasoning.md:20-22,40`). No training, fine-tuning, optimizer run, or neural inference is authorized by the historical examples or this specification, and the corpus is not training-qualified (`docs/specs/DS006-reasoning.md:40`; `docs/specs/DS018-source-rights.md:35-38`). External assets may be ingested/exported/published only if `docs/specs/DS018-source-rights.md` records them as `cleared` or `permissive-attribution` with their duties; QA2D data, ProofWriter, QQP and AmbigQA remain quarantined (`docs/specs/DS018-source-rights.md:23-35`). Historical demonstration answers and command listings in these chapters are not fresh execution evidence.
