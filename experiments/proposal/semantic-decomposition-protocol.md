# Semantic decomposition protocol: typed sub-goals over a numbered registry

**Archived 2026-10-03.** The code and data this document describes moved to `probably_obsolete/formalization-experiments-2026-10/` (its README gives the final numbers and the path map) when the owner decided to rebuild the formalization infrastructure. Paths below read as they did at the time.

Status: design proposal (planning agent Fable, 2026-10-02) for the owner's request of 2026-10-02: decompose problems semantically, not by sentence; find independent sub-problems, their types, type-specific questions, and connect the sub-results. To be implemented by an Opus agent as the next version of problem mode (`lib/query-author/step-by-step/problem.mjs`, `config/knowledge/formalizer-protocol-v1/0060-problem.sop`). No product code is changed by this document.

Starting point (eval/reports/current/books-eval/problem-mode.md, state/formalization-regression/*/summary.md): the step-by-step system solves 27/100 book problems with the 4B (bare 4B: 69); `good` answering the same problem questions reaches 4/20, so the questions and the assembly, not the model, are the bottleneck. The recurring defect is free-form naming: `problem_values` and `problem_facts` let the model invent names (`A | is_between_11_and_15`, a rule over `A_min_greater_than_B_max`) that the assembly cannot connect; `formula_wrong` (23/122) and `deduce_unknown` (18/122) are the largest clusters, and `problem_unreadable` grows (56/190) when the questions are reworded.

## 1. Theory: what applies and why

**Schema theory of word problems (Riley, Greeno & Heller 1983; Kintsch & Greeno 1985; Marshall 1995).** Arithmetic word problems fall into a small number of semantic schemas (change, combine, compare; Marshall adds group, restate, vary) with fixed slots (start, change, result; part, part, whole; referent, relation, compared). Solving is schema selection then slot filling; errors come from a wrong schema or a wrong slot, rarely from arithmetic. Kintsch & Greeno's "problem model" is built from the text's quantities and their roles, and Hegarty, Mayer & Monk (1995) showed that poor solvers use a direct-translation strategy (keywords to operations) while good solvers build a model of the quantities and relations first. This is the right unit for a 4B: schema selection is a closed choice, slot filling is a reference to a declared quantity, and both are checkable. The present `problem_values`/`problem_formulas` pair is direct translation (names invented from the text, then a formula over them); the registry below forces the problem-model strategy.

**Pólya (1945).** "What is the unknown? What are the data? What is the condition?" plus the auxiliary problem ("if you cannot solve the proposed problem, solve first a related problem") and looking back. These are closed questions a small model can answer separately: the unknowns are the goals (§3 N1), the data is the registry (N0), the condition is the type's slots (N3), the auxiliary problem is a derived quantity that becomes a sub-goal (N3, least-to-most), and looking back is the validator plus the two-signal checks.

**Least-to-most and decomposed prompting (Zhou et al. 2022; Khot et al. 2022; Dua et al. 2022 successive prompting; Wang et al. 2023 plan-and-solve).** Decomposition helps small models when the sub-questions are simple and the answers of earlier sub-questions are fed back explicitly; it hurts when the model must invent the decomposition in free text, which is the owner's experience with sentence splitting. The design keeps the benefit and removes the cost: the decomposition is driven by the system (goal, then type, then slots, then missing inputs), and the model only ever answers a closed question about one node.

**QDMR / Break (Wolfson et al. 2020) and MathQA operation programs (Amini et al. 2019).** A question is a short list of steps from a fixed operator set (select, filter, project, aggregate, comparative, superlative, arithmetic, boolean), each step referencing earlier steps by number (`#2`), and MathQA writes `add(n0, n1)` over the text's numbers by index. Two findings matter: a fixed operator inventory with references by index is learnable by small models, and the operators map one-to-one onto query-language constructs. SOP Lang's constructs (session predicate, `stated`, rule with `compute`/`compare`, `aggregate` sum/min/max/count, `rank`, `constraint`) are that operator set; the registry indices `v1..vn`, `e1..ek`, `d1..dm` are the `#k` references.

**Frame semantics (Fillmore; FrameNet) and coarse-to-fine semantic parsing (Dong & Lapata 2018).** A frame is a situation type with named frame elements; a parser first identifies the frame, then fills its elements. Our problem types are frames, their slots are frame elements, and the model does frame identification (a numbered choice) and element identification (which registry item fills which element) as two separate closed questions. Coarse-to-fine decoding (sketch first, details second) is the same discipline for program synthesis and is why the type is asked before any slot.

**Discourse representation theory (Kamp & Reyle 1993) and quantity schemas (Roy, Vieira & Roth 2015).** DRT introduces discourse referents once and refers to them afterwards; anaphora is resolution to a referent, not re-description. The registry is a referent table: every number and every named thing of the problem is a referent declared once. Roy et al.'s quantity schema (value, unit, the entity it counts, the verb/role) is the label the model attaches to each number. AMR and full DRT parses are not a fit for a 4B: they are open-vocabulary graph outputs with the same invented-name failure mode as free-form facts.

**Constraint-satisfaction modelling (variables, domains, constraints, objective).** Orderings, seatings, assignments by clues and the books' "Atomic Case: One Constraint Core" are one coupled model that must not be split: the lesson of those 100 items is that splitting "x ≥ 42", "x ≤ 55", "x multiple of 10" into sub-problems produces partial statements that mean nothing until recombined. The protocol therefore has an `atomic` type whose slots are all the constraints on the same variables, and the solver (an exact engine behind `constraint`) does the combination.

**GSM8K/MATH findings and program-aided reasoning (Cobbe et al. 2021; Gao et al. 2022 PAL; Chen et al. 2022 PoT).** Small models fail word problems mostly in planning the next operation and in binding numbers to roles, not in arithmetic; delegating execution to an interpreter removes the arithmetic errors entirely. ChatSOP is PAL with SOP Lang as the program and exact engines as the interpreter; the remaining error budget is binding (registry labels) and planning (type and slots), which is exactly what the closed questions target. Expert/novice categorization (Chi, Feltovich & Glaser 1981): experts classify problems by deep structure, novices by surface words; the type question is therefore phrased by the shape of the computation ("one value compared with a limit"), never by topic words.

**Probabilistic program induction and verb categorization (Hosseini et al. 2014 ARIS; Kushman et al. 2014 templates; Roy & Roth 2015).** Template-based solvers select an equation template and align its slots to the text's numbers; ARIS models state changes of containers by verb category. Both are the "type then slots" structure and show that a small, shared template inventory covers most of a grade-school corpus. The `state` type (start, changes, result; working backward) is ARIS's container model.

What suits a 4B answering closed questions: schema selection (type), slot filling by index, labelling of numbers with a short phrase, yes/no two-signal checks. What does not: free-form decomposition, free-form naming, writing rules over invented predicates, sentence segmentation.

## 2. Type inventory

Granularity: a type is one schema whose slots compile to one SOP fragment with one exact engine. A problem is a small graph of typed nodes; a node's output is a derived quantity or relation that other nodes reference by index. Types of the first inventory (the mapping is the assembler of §4; "exists" means the SOP construct is already admitted at the circuit surface, DS014 "Problems that state their own data"):

| type | asks for | slots (filled by index) | SOP mapping |
|---|---|---|---|
| `chain` | a number from given numbers | inputs (v/d), one formula over the slot names (`+ - * / // % ^ round ceil floor max min`) | session predicates, `stated` values, one rule with `compute` lines (exists) |
| `check` | yes/no: a value against a limit | left (v/d), relation (≤ < = ≥ > ≠, closed menu), right (v/d), optional `and` of checks | rule with `compare`, explicit `not` rule (exists) |
| `batch` | whole units needed, remainder, waves | demand (v/d), per-unit capacity (v/d), optional parallel factor | `rounded_up_to`, `whole_divided_by`, `modulo` (exists) |
| `choose` | which named thing is best | things (e), a score formula written once with role names, a table thing × role → v, direction (lowest/highest), optional feasibility checks that disqualify | `is_option`, per-option rules instantiated from the one formula, `option_score`, `rank`; a feasibility check adds `when not <check> 1` (exists; template instantiation is new assembly code) |
| `breakeven` | the volume where two linear costs meet, which is cheaper on each side | two (fixed, per-unit) pairs | `chain` with the closed-form `(f2 − f1) / (r1 − r2)` plus two `check` nodes at floor/ceil (exists) |
| `path_time` | earliest completion of tasks with precedence | tasks (e) with durations (v), precedence pairs (e before e), optional setup/buffer | `precedes` stated pairs, recursive earliest-finish rule with `maximum_with`, final `max` aggregate (exists since loop 3) |
| `route` | the feasible and fastest/cheapest route | routes (e), per-link times and capacities (v lists), required flow, time limit | per-route `aggregate sum` of times and `aggregate min` of capacities, `check` per route, `choose` (exists) |
| `order` | a complete or partial order from clues | things (e), clue pairs (e before/after e), positions (seat numbers, offsets) | `constraint` over integer positions with all-different (exists), or transitive `before` rules when only "who is first/between" is asked |
| `assign` | which case/thing satisfies all clues | things (e), properties with values (table e × property → value), clues (property = value) | stated properties, one rule per clue that derives `excluded`, answer = things not excluded (closed world; exists) |
| `deduce` | yes/no, who, how many from facts and if-then rules | things (e), a closed list of properties/relations declared first (`terms`), facts over them, rules over them, question | facts, rules, closed-world `follows` (exists; `terms` reader exists) |
| `abduce` | the best-supported hypothesis | hypotheses (e), observations (e or v), a table observation × hypothesis → supports/weakens/neutral | stated `supports`/`weakens`, `aggregate count` per hypothesis, `rank highest` on support minus weakening (new assembly over existing constructs) |
| `state` | the start or end state after changes | start (v or unknown), ordered changes (+v/−v), result (v or unknown) | `chain` forward, or the inverse chain for an unknown start (exists) |
| `count` | how many groups, pairs, members | sets (e), memberships (e in e), or numbers | `aggregate count` or `chain` (exists) |
| `atomic` | one variable constrained by several clauses | variables with domains, one constraint per clause, objective (possible/min/max) | `constraint` (exists; this is the puzzle kind of the general protocol) |
| `qualitative` | an explanation, a fallacy name, evidence sufficiency | none | honest `unclear` with the registry shown; escalation target, never a guess |

Left for later types, each a small variant: `pattern` (position in a repeating pattern: `modulo`), `spatial` (orientation relations as `deduce` with a fixed relation set), `interval` (low/high bounds as two `chain` nodes feeding a `check`), `probability` (frequency tables as `chain`).

## 3. The form: a typed sub-goal graph grown from the goals

Not a free decision tree over the text but a fixed node order whose branching is only the type menu; the graph of sub-goals is built from the goals backwards (what is asked, what it needs, what each need needs) and connected by indices. Independence is a result, not a question: two sub-goals are independent when their input index sets are disjoint, and SOP rules are declarative, so "facts intentionally out of solving order" costs nothing.

**N0 Registry (one structural pass, two labelling questions).**
- The system extracts every number of the message in order (structure: `numberMentioned`, decimals, percentages, thousands separators, number words up to twenty) as `v1..vn`, each with its verbatim span and the ten words around it (a window is structure, not interpretation).
- Q-R1, one line per number, shown with its window: `v3 (…each block takes 5 minutes…): what it is | of which thing | unit`. The model writes short labels; the reader keeps lines by index only, so a number cannot be invented and a label is never a predicate id (ids are `v3` plus a slug for rendering). A line `v7: irrelevant` is allowed and kept as a label (decorative numbers are common: staff roles, colour codes).
- Q-R2: "Which named things does the problem compare, rank, order or ask about? Copy each name as written, one per line." Reader: the existing message-contains check; `e1..ek`. For a table-shaped problem (options with the same roles), Q-R1's "of which thing" column already groups numbers by thing.
- Cost: 2 questions, ~300 tokens; the registry is the shared prefix of every later question (prompt cache).

**N1 Goals.** Q-G1: "What does the question ask for? One line per asked item: `g1: <what> | kind`", kind from a closed list (a number; yes or no; which named thing; an order of the things; a list; an explanation). A goal's label is for rendering only. Two-signal rule: the number of goals is checked against the number of question marks and "and" in the question sentence (structure); a disagreement asks once more with a hint, then keeps the model's answer.

**N2 Type per goal.** Q-T1, per goal, the numbered type menu with one-line shape definitions (never topic words): "1. a number computed from given numbers; 2. whether a value meets a limit; 3. how many whole units/batches are needed; 4. which named thing is best by a number computed for each; 5. the volume where two costs are equal; 6. the earliest finish of tasks that wait for each other; 7. the feasible and fastest route of several; 8. the order of things from before/after clues; 9. the thing that fits all clues; 10. what follows from facts and if-then rules; 11. the best-supported explanation; 12. a start or end state after changes; 13. how many groups, pairs or members; 14. one unknown number narrowed by several conditions; 15. an explanation or judgement that is not a computation". The goal kind restricts the admissible types (`fp_ptype_kind`): a yes/no goal cannot be type 1, a "which thing" goal cannot be type 2; a mismatch re-asks once with the admissible subset.

**N3 Slots per type (the type-specific questions).** Each type is a short script of closed questions over indices; every answer is read structurally:
- references: `v3`, `d2`, `e1` (or a number from the registry copied verbatim, mapped back to its index);
- menus: a comparator, a direction, `supports/weakens/neutral`;
- a formula over slot names (the existing `readFormula`, with names restricted to the type's slot names and indices);
- a table: rows = things, columns = roles, cells = indices (one line per row);
- `new: <label>`: a needed input that is not in the registry; the system declares `d_k`, adds a sub-goal of kind number for it and asks N2/N3 for that sub-goal (least-to-most; depth ≤ 3, ≤ 12 nodes, ≤ 24 questions per problem; beyond that the open sub-goal is reported as `unparsed`).

Examples of slot scripts:
- `check`: "Which value is tested? (an index or new:)", "Against which value? (index or new:)", "How? 1. at most 2. at least 3. equal 4. below 5. above", "Must another check also hold? (index of a goal, or none)".
- `choose`: "The things compared are e1 Option A, e2 Option B. Write the number each thing is judged by as one formula over role names you choose (like `setup + ceil(work / rate) * cycle`)", then the table "For each thing, which v is each role? `e1: setup = v2, work = v1, rate = v3, cycle = v4`", then direction, then "Must a thing meet a limit to be allowed? (a check goal index, or none)". One formula instantiated per option by the assembler removes the "options mapped to the same value" failure.
- `path_time`: "Tasks and durations: `e1 = v2` per line", "Which tasks must finish before which? `e2 after e1` per line", "Setup or buffer added once? (index or none)".
- `deduce`: the existing `terms` → facts → rules → question script, with every thing an `e` index and every term chosen from the declared list (the `declaredOnly` filter already exists); modus tollens is asked as a closed yes/no ("Does the problem also say what holds when the result is absent?").
- `abduce`: the hypotheses are `e`; one line per observation `o1: e1 weakens, e2 supports, e3 neutral`.
- `atomic`: the puzzle script of the general protocol (unknowns, conditions, aim) with the registry as the number source.

**N4 Assembly and connection.** The system writes one SOP fragment per node from its slots (the assemblers of §4) under one namespace: a registry item `v3` is the predicate `v3_<slug>` with its `stated` value, a derived quantity `d2` is the head of its node's rule, a thing `e1` is the entity string as written. Connection is by shared predicate ids, so the sub-goal graph is the rule dependency graph; the validator's existing checks (`compute_in_cycle`, `unbound_query_variable`, `duplicate_role`, `stated_duplicate`) are the connectability check, and a dangling reference (`d4` declared by `new:` but never given a node) is reported as `unparsed` with the goal held back, never guessed. Goals become queries in goal order; a goal of a `check` type is a yes/no query, of `choose` a ranked query plus the per-thing values, of `order` the solved constraint. The decision tree the owner describes is therefore the rule graph itself, and "connecting the sub-results" is the engine's job, not the model's.

**Understanding cost of finding sub-problems.** None beyond N1–N3: the model never splits text. Each `new:` answer is one more node; the depth bound keeps the cost linear in the number of derived quantities, which the decompose book shows is 2–10 and the other books 1–4. Expected questions per problem: 2 (registry) + 1 (goals) + per node 1 (type) + 2–4 (slots) ≈ 6 for a one-node problem, ≈ 14 for a five-node one.

**Escalation of a node to a larger tier.** A node, not the problem, is escalated when (a) its reader fails twice, (b) the two-signal check fails twice (goal kind vs type; number of things vs table rows), (c) the validator refuses the node's fragment after one repair round, or (d) the executor returns `unknown` for a derived value whose inputs are all stated (a formula over the wrong roles). Escalation re-asks the same question text with the same registry prefix on tier `small`, then `good`; the answer is recorded with the tier so the offline regression can attribute. Everything else about the problem stays on `tiny`. The type question of a long problem (> 120 words, > 10 numbers) may be routed to `small` up front by a protocol default when the stage-1 measurement shows `tiny` below 70% type agreement with `good`; this is one `default` wire, not a code rule.

## 4. Representation as base-memory data

The protocol layer stays `config/knowledge/formalizer-protocol-v1/` with the existing `fp_` conventions of 0060-problem.sop: question texts, formats, choices, token budgets, re-ask reminders and required/exit markers are facts; the greedy and the IR controller read them; the structural readers and assemblers are named by data and live in code. Existing predicates reused unchanged: `fp_question_text`, `fp_answer_format`, `fp_choice`, `fp_choice_text`, `fp_choices_from`, `fp_follow_up`, `fp_problem_tokens`, `fp_problem_again`, `fp_problem_required`, `fp_problem_step_when`, `fp_hint`, `fp_example`, `fp_establishes`, `fp_priority`, and the action/rule pattern `fp_ask_problem`. New data predicates (predicate wires, not wire types), in a new file `0070-decomposition.sop`:

```
@fp_ptype predicate              args subject:entity                         # a problem type of the inventory
@fp_ptype_text predicate         args subject:entity object:text             # its one-line shape definition in the type menu
@fp_ptype_kind predicate         args subject:entity object:entity           # a goal kind the type may answer (number, yesno, which, order, list)
@fp_ptype_step predicate         args subject:entity object:integer topic:entity   # slot question `topic` is step `object` of type `subject`
@fp_slot_from predicate          args subject:entity object:entity           # the slot question draws its options from registry|things|derived|goals|menu|formula|table
@fp_slot_many predicate          args subject:entity                         # the slot takes several lines
@fp_slot_new predicate           args subject:entity                         # the slot may answer `new:` and open a sub-goal
@fp_ptype_assembler predicate    args subject:entity object:entity           # the structural assembler of the type (named like fp_answer_format names readers)
@fp_tree_budget predicate        args subject:entity object:integer          # max_depth 3, max_nodes 12, max_questions 24
@fp_escalate_when predicate      args subject:entity object:entity           # node condition → tier (reader_failed_twice small, validator_refused good, ...)
@fp_registry_window predicate    args subject:entity object:integer          # words of context shown around a number (10)
```

The registry, goal and type questions are `fp_question_text` facts with `{{registry}}`, `{{things}}`, `{{goals}}`, `{{types}}` placeholders filled from the node state (structure). For InternalReasoningStepByStep the nodes are planned actions: `fp_open_goal ?g`, `fp_goal_type ?g ?t`, `fp_open_slot ?g ?s`, `fp_derived_pending ?d` are the slots; `fp_ask_type`, `fp_ask_slot` and `fp_escalate` are actions with `requires`/`adds`/`cost`, the goal `fp_formalized` holds when no goal has an open slot and the assembled circuit validated; the learned-rules layer (`formalizer-learned-v1`) may add `fp_hint`/`fp_example` lines per slot question and per type, which is the automated improver's lever.

SOP fragments per type are code assemblers in version 1 (`arithmeticCircuit` and `deductionCircuit` already exist; `choose` instantiation, `path_time`, `route`, `assign`, `abduce` are new functions of the same kind, structure only). Version 2 moves them into the protocol memory as host-approved `template` wires (`params`, `yield`, `body`; expanded by the trusted runtime through `expand`, never by the model): the per-type SOP body becomes reviewable data and a new type is a template plus its `fp_ptype*` facts, no code. The `template` wire exists (`docs/wire_typs/template.html`); whether its expansion fits session circuits of a chat turn is the one implementation question to settle before version 2.

Wire-type needs: none for stages A–C below. `abduce` on the model surface is written with ordinary session predicates (`supports`, `weakens`) and aggregates; when the owner wants the reason packet to name hypotheses and evidence as such (metacognition), that is the argument/hypothesis surface already noted in problem-mode.md "What still blocks" 1 and belongs in `experiments/proposal/wire-type-proposals.md`, not here.

## 5. Evaluation plan

Preregister as `eval-semantic-decomposition-v1` (DS007; `status/experiments.json`). Arms: the decomposition protocol on `tiny`; the same tree on `good` (every node answered by `good`, no escalation) as the question-quality ceiling; the current problem questions on `tiny` as the control. Sample: stratified by the books' section/tag metadata into the type strata of §2 (every type with ≥ 10 rows gets 10, qualitative 5 as a control for honest unknowns), never a row already in `eval/formalization-regression/cases.jsonl` or `datasets_sources/books/eval/seen.jsonl`.

Stages and stopping (AGENTS.md "Early stopping"): stage 1 = 10 per stratum (≈ 150 problems) on `tiny` and `good`; stop a stratum early when the paired bootstrap interval of correct(decomposition) − correct(control) is decisively positive or futile; fix generically (question text, menu, reader, assembler, hint wires) between stages, rerun a fresh 10 per stratum; only then stage 2 = 25 per stratum, once. Report, per stratum and per tier: correct / wrong / unknown / invalid; questions per problem; nodes per problem (against the decompose book's stated sub-problem count: a direct check of the decomposition itself); escalations per node kind; type agreement `tiny` vs `good`; and the first diverging node.

Offline replay regression: record every node's question hash and answer per tier (the existing record/replay cache, `lib/formalize/replay-cache.mjs`) and extend `tools/eval/formalization-regression/offline.mjs` with the node kinds (`registry`, `goals`, `type:<g>`, `slot:<g>:<s>`, `assembly`, `validator`, `execute`); the counterfactual swap already there (the reference tier's answers for the first k steps) attributes a failure to the first node whose `tiny` answer differs in meaning from `good`'s. Expectations come from the worked solutions without copying text: the numbers in a solution are a multiset of intermediate and final values, so "intermediate recall" = the share of a node's derived values that appear in the solution, and the decompose book's `SPk` count is the expected node count; a case passes only when the final answer matches and no derived value contradicts the solution's numbers. The gate for admitting any change is the existing one: fixes ≥ 1, losses = 0 on the accumulated regression set; the 122-case baseline is replayed under the new protocol first, so the old recordings keep their value where the question text is unchanged (the deduce script) and are re-recorded where it is not.

## 6. Risks and staged rollout

Risks: (1) registry labelling errors (a price read as a count) move the old formula errors one step earlier; mitigated by the context window per number and by the table question of `choose` (roles are named by the formula, then mapped), and measured by registry agreement with `good`. (2) Over-splitting coupled constraints; mitigated by the `atomic` type and by never asking the model to split. (3) Budget growth on ten-part problems (≤ 24 questions, ~2 min on `tiny`); acceptable for the benchmark, and the prompt cache holds the registry prefix. (4) Type menu drift: fifteen choices is at the edge for a 4B; the goal-kind filter shortens the menu to 3–6 per goal, and the stage-1 agreement with `good` decides whether the type question moves to `small` by default. (5) Readers tightened by index may raise `problem_unreadable`; every reader keeps one re-ask with the format reminder and the escalation path. (6) The qualitative fifth of the corpus stays unknown by design; it is reported apart so it never hides numeric progress.

Rollout, by expected gain per implementation effort (counts from §2):
- **Stage A** (first win, ~2,400 rows across commonsense, decompose, adult, world): `chain`, `check`, `batch`, `choose` with `breakeven` as its closed-form variant, over the registry and the goals; this replaces `problem_values`/`problem_formulas`/`problem_options`/`problem_asked` and reuses `arithmeticCircuit`. Success criterion: commonsense and decompose above the bare 4B on the numeric strata.
- **Stage B** (~650 rows): `path_time` and `route` (sum/min/max aggregates and the recursive earliest-finish rule exist).
