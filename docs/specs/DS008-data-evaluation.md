## Introduction

Dataset builders and evaluators use sourced, reviewed [semantic cases](wiki.html#definition-semantic-case) to check whether a natural-language request and its candidate [SOP](wiki.html#definition-sop) program have the same intended meaning. A seed program passing a symbolic execution check is not a trained model prediction, independently human-validated answer, or proof of broad language understanding.

## Core Content

### Data rights and provenance

Record raw source, license and redistribution rights, source location/version, transformations, generator/model identity when used, prompt/revision, reviewer decision, and executable target. The software repository's license does not license QA2D, ProofWriter, QQP, PAWS/PAWS-X, AmbigQA, AmbigNQ, model weights, or derivative datasets. Rights are recorded per asset in [DS014-source-rights.md](specsLoader.html?spec=DS014-source-rights.md): by the owner's decision of 2026-09-28 the corpora inspired by QQP, PAWS, ProofWriter, AmbigNQ, QA2D and SQuAD are released as inspiration only. They take structure, label types, phenomena and statistics from their sources, never text, and every exported row must pass `node tools/datasets/no-copy.mjs`. Source text itself stays in the raw cache `datasets_sources/` and is ingested, exported or published only where DS014 records it as cleared or permissive-attribution. For ingesting materials, preserve source quotations and distinguish source knowledge, reusable implicit proposals, problem state and reasoner infrastructure; only reviewed material enters approved knowledge ([DS011-material-sources.md](specsLoader.html?spec=DS011-material-sources.md)).

### Authoring contract

Corpora are JSONL-first. The authoring source of the model-language corpora is the diversity generator under `tools/datasets/diversity/` ([DS022](specsLoader.html?spec=DS022-diversity-generator.md)); `node tools/datasets/build-corpora.mjs` executes every gold against its verification world and writes the JSONL splits `datasets_archive/formalizer-v1/{train,dev}.jsonl` (sharded above 45 MB through `lib/jsonl-shards.mjs`), the sealed `eval/suites/formalizer-v1/test.jsonl` and the out-of-distribution suite `eval/suites/formalizer-ood-v1/test.jsonl`. A case is changed in the generator and the splits are rebuilt; the JSONL files are never hand-edited. Human review uses the visual corpus audit and its verdict ledger, and `node tools/datasets/audit-corpus.mjs --corpus <name>` checks the fail-closed structural invariants plus faithfulness, context-triviality, diversity and template-leakage checks, writing `eval/reports/current/corpus-audit/<corpus>.json` ([DS020](specsLoader.html?spec=DS020-corpus-audit-tool.md)). Neither a passing audit nor a recorded verdict makes a corpus a qualified training dataset.

Rows carry the stable `split_group_id`, the generator `source` revision, the `lineage` (inspiration source, family, variant) and the rights stamp. These are reproducibility labels, **not** reviewed semantics: `review_status` remains `not_reviewed`, and `quality_flags.human_reviewed` and `quality_flags.training_approved` remain false. A parse or runtime pass cannot establish independent human semantic adjudication.

### Case schema

`tools/datasets/schema.mjs:validateRecord/validateCorpus` defines the earlier sourced-case row format, still used by `tools/datasets/semantic-compare.mjs` and the training controller's checks. Row keys include `id`, `semantic_case_id`, `split_group_id`, `structure_id`, `surface_group_id`, `split`, `question`, `sop_target`, `setup_sop`, `evaluation_track`, `language`, `semantic_status`, `negative_of`, `context_assertions`, `source`, `generation_trace`, `quality_flags`, `context`, `expected`; optional `input_mode` is normally explicit. `source` requires `id`, `kind`, `uri`, `revision`, `sha256`, `license` (and validates the `content` checksum when present); `expected.status` is an oracle, not a prediction. `context` fields are validated in `schema.mjs` (`now`, `language`, `entities`, `predicates`, `approvedTemplates`, `procedures_sop`); they are host and evaluator scaffolding, never model input. In legacy-format rows `context_assertions` (not model input) and `generation_trace` preserve the difference between authored input, source provenance and oracle.

The model-language corpora of DS022 carry the same identity, split and oracle fields (`id`, `semantic_case_id`, `split_group_id`, `structure_id`, `split`, `question`, `sop_target`, `evaluation_track`, `language`, `expected`, `quality_flags`) plus `family`, `question_type`, the compact rights stamp and the evaluation-only verification world (`ontology_sop`, `world`, `setup_sop`, `late_setup_sop`); `tools/datasets/verify-corpus.mjs` checks them fail-closed against the corpus manifest and re-executes the targets, and `tests/data/formalizer-corpus.test.mjs` runs it.

### Separation and semantic checks

Keep all paraphrases, translations, and linked contrasts of one `semantic_case_id` in the same train/dev/test partition; group semantic duplicates even when identifiers differ. Reserve composition-level holdouts before generation. Primary English and stratified independent Romanian checks have different coverage obligations; neither guarantees transfer to every dialect or unseen linguistic construction. Check syntax, types, symbols, permission, canonical form, relevant-world execution, paraphrase invariance, negative contrasts, and temporal/negation fidelity separately. Executing the gold SOP checks the program against chosen worlds, not the model's first-shot formalization on unseen language.

`node tools/datasets/build-corpora.mjs` deterministically writes the model-language corpora (DS022): train/dev rows under `datasets_archive/formalizer-v1/`, the physically separate sealed test under `eval/suites/formalizer-v1/`, the out-of-distribution suite, manifests with logical-file hashes, and reserved test resources. `node tools/datasets/verify-corpus.mjs --corpus formalizer-v1 --all` checks the corpus, hashes, split boundaries and the executed outcome of every row against its verification world in an isolated repository. Passing these checks approves generated fixture consistency only; it does not make a source human-reviewed, a source license verified, or a sampled model accurate.

ML projections are **instruction-free**. The prompt is exactly the user's message — `barePrompt` from `eval/llm.mjs` — and a row carries the whole message in `question`; `setup_sop`, `ontology_sop`, `context`, `expected` and any per-row lexicon are verification scaffolding for the host and the evaluator, never model input ([DS021](specsLoader.html?spec=DS021-model-surface.md)); the evaluator rebuilds the prompt from the message and ignores a stored `row.prompt`. `tools/research/prepare-experiment.mjs` writes the projection (`datasets_archive/formalizer-v1/formalizer/`, `prompt_profile: message-only`) and refuses to write when any prompt differs from its message; the Python row loader rejects a projection without that profile. The operator decision: tiny SFT models must learn SOP Lang from examples; the long instruction text stays in exactly one place, `server/prompts/formalizer.txt`, used only when serving untrained/base models conversationally. Serving a model fine-tuned on this input format must use the same instruction-free input, not `formalPrompt`. `tests/model-input-boundary.test.mjs` recomputes the projection. The pinned Qwen3-0.6B tokenizer audit recorded on the earlier pilot (105 train and 44 dev rows, maxima 383/502 tokens against a 4,096 limit) is historical; the projection of `formalizer-v1` has not been token-audited yet.

The model target for this projection is declarative SOP only. It is written as strings: `stated` reports what the message puts forward, `assumed` what the model adds, `unclear` an unintelligible message (`gibberish`), one without a statement or question (`no_request`) or one with several equally good readings (`ambiguous`, with its readings), and `query` (with `match` blocks)/`constraint` state problems; the evaluator reports stated/assumed separation and `basis` coverage and accuracy separately from formalization accuracy, which ignores `basis` ([DS021](specsLoader.html?spec=DS021-model-surface.md)). The host-generated circuit and its effects are separately evaluated system artifacts; the model does not select `remember`, `solve`, `cnl`, or `clarify`. When a specified problem has missing/ambiguous identity or a required downstream scalar is unavailable/nonunique, symbolic orchestration generates an inspectable clarification, rather than training the model to write one. The earlier tokenizer audit measured the old projection, not automatically a newly generated declarative target set. Raw input quotations remain provenance on the host side, not model-controlled `source` or `quote` fields.

### Split policy and observed overlap

**CI-enforced semantic boundary:** `validateCorpus` checks that every surface of a `semantic_case_id`, every `split_group_id` and every `negative_of` contrast remains inside one train/dev/test partition; it computes their transitive connected components. A family or structure label alone does not establish semantic identity. Existing manifests intentionally split by world and allow templates to recur across splits. **Advisory/opt-in:** `validateFamilyStructureIsolation(rows)` demands stronger family-or-structure component isolation for a new collection; applying it to a corpus that splits by world fails, as expected. Do not misreport that failure as semantic-case leakage or rewrite the sealed exports.

The model-language corpora group every surface, paraphrase, noise variant and contrast of one semantic case under one `split_group_id` and keep each group inside one split; `tools/datasets/verify-corpus.mjs` checks that boundary against the manifest. Template-level overlap between splits is measured, not forbidden, and is reported by the corpus audit (see "Target faithfulness, template leakage and the sealed-test boundary" below). The per-family overlap measured on the deleted query curriculum is archived in [DS009-query-curriculum.md](specsLoader.html?spec=DS009-query-curriculum.md).

### Evaluation tracks

The `formalization` track measures model-authored `stated`, `assumed`, `unclear`, `query`, and `constraint` declarations: syntax/schema validity, semantic fidelity, stated/assumed separation, faithful strings, omissions, inventions and certainty. A `stated` or `assumed` proposition is lowered by the host to a turn-local fact, not an observed fact or session write. The host compiles declarations into inspectable retrieval/solve circuitry and generates `clarify` only for unresolved identity or required scalar inputs. Do not ask the small model to choose `remember`, `pack`, `resolve`, `solve`, or `cnl` steps. Exclude `jsEval`, complex code and coding-agent programs from model targets. Use independently reviewed held-out language and gold meaning; executing a symbolic gold circuit tests the host/runtime, not the model.

The separate `system` track measures complete trusted circuits: memory acquisition/retrieval and isolation, reasoning and proofs, approved procedures (including `jsEval` where a coding agent supplies the JavaScript), actual backend/fallback, temporal behavior, provenance, and end-to-end outcomes and latency. System-only examples and their `system/*.jsonl` exports cannot be passed to the model endpoint. A correct system answer does not by itself demonstrate model formalization fidelity, and a correct gold-program execution demonstrates neither.

Previously generated suites and reports contain mixed executable-circuit targets and measurements; historical scores are never relabelled as declarative-model scores. Atom grammar is whitespace-only and the explicit session recording wire is `remember`. Active derived suites are rebuilt from their authorship sources before they are treated as executable, while historical measured artifacts keep their original provenance. Dataset migration alone does not authorize model training.

Reports identify track, syntax/capability version, suite/provenance and model or system configuration; they state the number of eligible cases, attempted cases, missing/invalid outputs, abstentions, operational exclusions, and every metric's numerator and denominator. Small-model reports separate all-eligible syntax/schema and semantic/effects/error rates from conditional rates on valid predictions, with language/family/source slices and representative failures. Whole-system reports separately count end-to-end task successes/failures, memory and temporal/provenance errors, actual backend/fallback/skips, unauthorized effects, and stage/end-to-end latency, with per-slice denominators and controlled component comparisons. The two tracks are never pooled into one accuracy figure, and a skipped backend never counts as a pass. Metric definitions are in [DS016-evaluation-metrics.md](specsLoader.html?spec=DS016-evaluation-metrics.md).

### Clean-English partition (formalization scope)

By owner decision of 2026-09-30 (journaled), the primary formalization evaluation focuses on **valid, clean English**; Romanian, mixed Romanian/English and badly written English (typos, garbled or confusing input) leave the formalization evaluation and are handled by a separate `textToCleanEnglish` chat-UI service under user control. `tools/datasets/clean-english.mjs` sorts every row of `datasets_archive/formalizer-v1` and its three sealed suites (`eval/suites/formalizer-v1`, `eval/suites/formalizer-ood-v1`, `eval/suites/formalizer-wild-v1`) into one of four partitions, in this precedence: `mixed` (a `code_switch` tag, or LanguagesUtil's `identify()` calling the unquoted message `mixed`), `ro` (`language: ro`, not mixed), `clean_en` (`language: en`, not mixed, and every clean-English gate passes), `noisy_en` (`language: en`, not mixed, at least one gate fails). The clean-English gate is re-checkable from a row's `question` text and metadata alone, independent of the generator's own tags: (a) no generator-injected noise/typo/code-switch tag; (b) `lib/languages-util/spellfix.mjs` proposes no typo correction (a proposal that only splits a compound, switches British and American spelling or concerns a capitalized name is not a typo); (c) every word token outside quoted spans, proper names (capitalized tokens) and numbers is labelled `en` by LanguagesUtil's `identify()`, unless the English dictionary also lists it; (d) `eval/reference-free.mjs` `gibberishVerdict()` is not `gibberish`; (e) a local grammar check, not implemented (no local grammar checker is installed; recorded as a `TODO.md` follow-up, never silently substituting for gates (a)-(d)). A short, well-formed English fragment is not penalized for length.

`node tools/datasets/build-clean-english.mjs` writes the train and dev side of the corpus **clean-english**, `datasets_archive/clean-english/{train,dev}.jsonl` (clean_en rows of formalizer-v1 train/dev, split preserved), and never opens a sealed file (a generator may not, AGENTS.md rule 9); `node tools/eval/clean-english-suite.mjs` (run after it) writes the sealed `eval/suites/clean-english/test.jsonl` and patches the test fields of the corpus manifest. The test file (clean_en rows of the three sealed suites, each tagged `suite` and `source_id`, id `"<suite>::<source_id>"`); rows keep their original verification scaffolding (`world`, `ontology_sop`, `setup_sop`) unchanged, so `formalizer-v1`/`formalizer-ood-v1` rows remain executable through `eval/run.mjs` `evaluate()` and `formalizer-wild-v1` rows (no verification world) remain scored reference-free through `tools/eval/wild-suite.mjs` `scoreAgainstAccepted`, exactly as their source suite already required. It introduces no new source text, so it inherits `formalizer-v1`'s rights record rather than declaring a new one (`datasets/SOURCES.md` "clean-english"). Partition manifests (ids and counts only, for later separate evaluation) for `noisy_en`, `ro` and `mixed` live at `datasets_archive/formalizer-v1/partitions/*.json` (train/dev) and `eval/reports/current/clean-english/partitions/<suite>.json` (sealed suites).

The filter's precision was checked by three manual spot-checks of 60 accepted and 60 rejected rows each (`status/notes/clean-english.jsonl`, topic `clean-english`; details in `datasets/SOURCES.md` "clean-english"). They caught a keyboard-mash row slipping through as `clean_en` (fixed by gate (d)) and a systematic over-rejection of clean English that contained Romanian-looking first names, English words that Romanian also lists, British spellings or compounds (fixed by deviation D1: gate (c) ignores capitalized tokens and dictionary English words, gate (b) ignores compound splits, British/American variants and capitalized names). No noisy or non-English text was found among the accepted rows of the last check; a few jargon-heavy wild rows ("config", "repo") remain falsely rejected, which costs `clean_en` recall only.

### Three datasets (owner decision of 2026-09-30)

The first level of `datasets/` holds three dataset folders, each with one purpose, plus the owner-message collection `natural` (next sections); the legacy corpora that feed them (`formalizer-v1`, `proofing`, `proofing-diverse-dev`, `clean-english`, `diversity`) are provenance inputs and the gold-SOP arbiter, kept unchanged in the tracked folder `datasets_archive/` (`lib/dataset-paths.mjs` resolves the old `datasets/<legacy>/...` paths stored in frozen manifests, preregistrations and sealed rows; `datasets_archive/PATH_ALIASES.json` lists them). Splits are `datasets/<name>/{train,dev}.jsonl` and the sealed `eval/suites/<name>/test.jsonl`; each dataset has a `manifest.json` (format `chatsop-dataset-manifest-v3`: counts, sha256, sources, the SymbolicLM code hashes and the Stanza model id) and a `README.md` with the rules below in operational form. Nothing in them is human-reviewed and none is authorized for training.

* **`bad_english`**: messages that are not clean English (Romanian, mixed Romanian/English, spelling and grammar problems, garbled text), each with an acceptable clean-English `target` where one is known. Material to evaluate and later fine-tune **LanguageProofingLLM**, the model behind the chat's `textToCleanEnglish` step (translation plus spelling and grammar repair into acceptable English). Rule: the clean-English classifier (`tools/datasets/clean-english.mjs`, previous section) says `ro`, `mixed` or `noisy_en` (`language_kind`), plus new cases (`datasets_sources/new_cases`) whose message differs from its clean reference because of noise (classifier says not clean, or a `typos`/`casual_register` tag); a new case its author declares clean is never noisy on a `mixed` verdict alone. Target, in order: an oracle-checked `proofing` repair of exactly that message; the clean text rebuilt by undoing the recorded generator noise operations; a clean sibling of the same semantic case with the same gold SOP; for Romanian and mixed rows the oracle-checked English rendering of the same semantic case; the new case's clean reference. Every target passes the clean-English gate; rows without target stay, flagged `no_target`.
* **`symbolic_english`**: correct English whose **SymbolicLM** grammatical analysis is correct (Stanza parse plus UD rules; owner definition: the analysis is what is judged, the SOP is a later layer). Each row stores the message, SymbolicLM's grammatical analysis (`analysis`: the UD parse per sentence, tokens `[id, form, lemma, upos, head, deprel]`) and, for the later layer, the SOP Lang output with its comparison to the gold (`sop_layer`). It is a **regression suite**: whenever SymbolicLM, the Stanza model or the engine changes, every row must still be analysed the same or better (`node tools/symbolic-regression.mjs`; classes `same`, `analysis_changed_sop_same`, `sop_changed_equivalent`, `sop_changed`, `now_failing`; non-zero exit on the last two; `--update` re-baselines explicitly), and the inventory of forms SymbolicProofingLLM rewrites into. **Membership (analysis layer; owner direction of 2026-09-30 night, the same for every clean-English row, with a gold SOP or without):** a row is in `symbolic_english` when EVERY sentence of its message passes the calibrated analysis gate: (1) the Stanza default-package tree and the accurate-package tree of the sentence are identical (head and relation of every word; `compareSentence` class `identical`, `lib/symbolic-lm/uncertainty.mjs`; a different sentence split is a difference), and (2) the DeepSeek parse judge (`tools/research/parse-judge.mjs` conditions a and c, run from omp task folders under `datasets_sources/`) says good enough (CORRECT, MINOR or INPUT_TYPO) on both conditions; every other clean-English row is in `neuro_english`. The judge is `DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash`, calibrated against a stronger reference (`datasets_sources/parse_judge_deepseek/`, journal "DeepSeek flash calibrated as a UD parse judge": identical trees and both conditions good reached 97.8% precision on an enriched sample of 136 sentences). A judge verdict judges a tree, not an SOP, and is keyed on the sentence text plus its tree, so a verdict recorded for the same sentence and tree in any task folder (`neuro_oracle_parse_judge`, `symbolic_proofing_parse_judge`, `backgen_parse_judge`, `parse_judge_deepseek`, `resplit_parse_judge`) is reused and only missing ones are judged (`tools/datasets/three-datasets/analysis-gate.mjs`; a missing parse or verdict is `pending`, never a pass). Rows the gate cannot judge follow the SOP rules: a message with no analysed sentence (a greeting answered before parsing, gibberish, a crash) or with an `unparsed` span is here when its SOP is verified (a strict match with the gold under the rules `ud-rules-v2.5` and the Stanza `accurate` package, `eval-symbolic-accurate-adopt-v1`, or, without a gold, a valid SOP with a real outcome and no unparsed span) and in `neuro_english` otherwise. `analysis_verified` is `analysis_gate` or `sop_rule` (`gold_sop_match` is a retired name; production cases of `tools/datasets/add-case.mjs` use the same two values, plus `analysis_pending_judge` while a verdict is missing); `analysis_verdict` holds the gate result per sentence (tree class, verdicts a and c, the folder each verdict came from) and its reasons; `sop_layer` is `{status: match|mismatch|no_gold, handled, failure_kind, failure}` (a row whose SOP misses its gold keeps the gold and its SOP-layer blame `parser`/`rules`/`gold_convention`/`unknown` there); `verification` = `{sop_gold_match, judge, stanza_spacy_agree, stanza_default_accurate}`: `stanza_default_accurate` is the worst class `identical`, `noncore_diff` or `core_diff` over the sentences between the default package's tree (cache `analysis-default-v1.6/`, else the recorded default parses) and the current one (`core_diff`: a differing head or relation on a core arc: root, subjects, objects, obliques, clausal complements and modifiers, conjunction, copula, auxiliary, mark, or the head of a prepositional phrase); `judge` holds the verdicts per sentence; spaCy agreement on the core arcs is a weak second opinion. Until the incident of 2026-09-30 night the builders used the SOP match as a proxy (a row with a gold SOP entered only if SymbolicLM's SOP matched it, and the no-gold rows passed a Haiku gate); every number and experiment computed on that split is marked "computed on the SOP-proxy split" (`status/experiments.json`), and the movement of the rows is in `eval/reports/current/three-datasets/resplit-summary.md`. The earlier Haiku gate (`eval-symbolic-gate-v1`; its tools `symbolic-gate.mjs`, `symbolic-gate-report.mjs` and `gate.mjs` are archived under `probably_obsolete/legacy/tools/symbolic-gate/`) and its report `symbolic-gate-report.md` are history for membership; the clusters of analysis skeletons stay in `symbolic-forms-inventory.md`.
* **`neuro_english`**: grammatically correct English whose **SymbolicLM** grammatical analysis is not correct, with a meaning-preserving rewrite whose analysis passes the gate where known: fine-tuning and evaluation material for **SymbolicProofingLLM** (the small fine-tuned model that transfers correct English into the limited English SymbolicLM understands without losing semantic equivalence). Rule: clean English and at least one sentence that fails the analysis gate above (default and accurate trees differ, or the DeepSeek parse judge says not good enough on condition a, c or both), or, for a message the gate cannot judge, a failed SOP rule. Per row `failure_kind` is the ANALYSIS failure: `trees_differ`, `judge_ac`, `judge_a`, `judge_c` (the first that any sentence has, in this order; all reasons are in `failure.reasons` and `analysis_verdict.reasons`), `no_analysis` or `unparsed_span` (`analysis_verified: sop_rule_failed`), or `pending_judge` (a verdict still missing; never in a finished build). The SOP layer of the row, with the failure kinds of the earlier builds (`parser`, `rules`, `gold_convention`, `unknown`: the proofing layer of a rewrite the current engine still verifies, otherwise the structural diff classes P, R, C and host frame normalization), is in `sop_layer` for the later layer; every neuro row is `rewrite_target: true` (the analysis failed). Targets: `proofing` repairs of exactly the message and meaning-preserving attempts of the symbolic-layers study that the **current** engine analyses to the gold SOP (strict; the recorded oracle of the older rules no longer counts), and the LLM-written clean reference of a new case when SymbolicLM handles it (pending human review); these target checks are SOP-layer checks. The rewrite candidates of the SymbolicProofingLLM pair set are verified on the analysis layer (below); rows nobody can rewrite yet stay, flagged `no_target`; a target that differs from the message only in casing, punctuation or spacing is flagged `formatting_only` (a rule can make that fix, so the row is evidence for a rule before it is training material). The extension of SymbolicLM by rules and repairs comes first; rewriting covers what rules cannot fix.

**Policy (canonical names and rules in [DS021](specsLoader.html?spec=DS021-model-surface.md) "Names, roles and datasets").** Formalization is evaluated on clean English. The priority is improving SymbolicLM by rules and repairs; a rewrite (SymbolicProofingLLM) is only for what rules cannot fix. LanguageProofingLLM is currently the fine-tuned Gemma 3 270M, iteration 1 (`eval/reports/history/language-proofing-it1/summary.md`); the base to try first for both proofing models is Gemma 3 270M. There is no fine-tuning until the owner approves after reviewing the three datasets in the audit page (DS020, one tab per dataset). The `proofreader` of earlier records is SymbolicProofingLLM; its training role id and run path are frozen.

Every row keeps its provenance (`source`: corpus, id, suite, split, semantic case, family, question type; `split_group_id` (new cases: `new_cases:<writer>`); `rights` per DS014 via `tools/datasets/rights.mjs`; `quality_flags`; `review_status`, `reviewed-by:pending` for the LLM-written new cases). Split policy: the source splits by group (formalizer-v1 groups; new cases by writer, the sealed writers of `datasets_sources/new_cases/split-proposal.json` as the test and four deterministic dev writers), so no group crosses a split; rows of the sealed suites `eval/suites/formalizer-v1` and `proofing` are only in the sealed test of the dataset they land in (rule 9), while the legacy out-of-distribution and wild suites (`formalizer-ood-v1`, `formalizer-wild-v1`) are archive and provenance whose forms are learning material: by the owner decision of 2026-09-30 (which supersedes the earlier rule that their rows go only to the test) `node tools/eval/legacy-resplit.mjs` splits them by split group (a wild row is its own group) into 70% train, 15% dev and 15% test with a fixed seed, writes the train and dev parts to `datasets_archive/legacy-resplit/<suite>/{train,dev}.jsonl` (the builders read them like any legacy corpus) and leaves the legacy files unchanged; their test part is sealed by `tools/eval/three-datasets-suites.mjs`; the sealed proofing test is made of formalizer-v1 dev messages, so the formalizer-v1 dev rows of the split groups it uses are sealed as test here too (`source.origin_split: dev`, `source.sealed_by`) and are not dev; a message whose normalized text equals a sealed message, or a dev message, is dropped from train. `tools/datasets/build-three-datasets.mjs` builds train and dev and never opens a sealed file; `tools/eval/three-datasets-suites.mjs` builds the sealed tests and the sealed-text hashes; `tools/datasets/verify-three-datasets.mjs` (run by `node check-datasets.mjs`) checks manifests, hashes, schema, splits, the sealed boundary and targets. The SymbolicLM analysis is cached per message text under `eval/reports/current/three-datasets/analysis/` (the cache of the default package under rules v1.6 is kept in `analysis-default-v1.6/` for the gate's tree comparison, and the spaCy agreement of unchanged trees is reused); `analyze()` returns it as `analysis` (`lib/symbolic-lm/index.mjs`, backward compatible). Full rebuild, in order: `node tools/eval/three-datasets-suites.mjs hashes`; `node tools/datasets/build-three-datasets.mjs analyze [--shard i/n] [--threads t]`, then again with `--targets`; `node tools/eval/three-datasets-suites.mjs analyze`, then `targets`; `node tools/datasets/build-three-datasets.mjs spacy`; `node tools/datasets/build-three-datasets.mjs gate-stage` and `node tools/eval/three-datasets-suites.mjs gate-stage` (record the default-package and accurate-package parses the analysis gate lacks, GPU, one worker, and append the judge items still missing to `datasets_sources/resplit_parse_judge/`); the DeepSeek judge task over that folder (`setsid nohup omp -p --model "DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash" --auto-approve --no-title @datasets_sources/resplit_parse_judge/TASK.md "<instruction>" > …/logs/omp-run.log 2>&1 < /dev/null &`, system prompts `SYSTEM_a.txt` and `SYSTEM_c.txt` generated from `tools/research/parse-judge.mjs`); `node tools/datasets/build-three-datasets.mjs assemble [--datasets symbolic_english,neuro_english]` and `node tools/eval/three-datasets-suites.mjs assemble [--datasets symbolic_english,neuro_english]` (a row without a verdict is written flagged `pending_judge`, so run the judge until none is left); `node tools/datasets/build-three-datasets.mjs report` (reuse analysis, coverage, README counts) and `node tools/datasets/build-three-datasets.mjs resplit-report` (before and after counts, movement of the rows, judge cost). The analysis step runs Stanza on the GPU in batches of 64 when one is visible (about 5 texts per second with the accurate package; `CHATSOP_UD_DEVICE=cpu` for the CPU); the cache is regenerable and not tracked.

### Content-word overlap (evaluation is an engineering check)

Owner decision of 2026-09-30: evaluation is an engineering check, not a proof that the system or a small model discovers new grammar. A test may repeat a grammatical **form** of the training side; it must not repeat a **case**. `node tools/datasets/audit/content-word-overlap.mjs` compares every sealed test row of each of the three datasets with the train and dev rows of the same dataset and writes `eval/reports/current/three-datasets/content-word-overlap.json`:

* **(a) Text duplicates.** An exact or normalized (case, diacritics, punctuation and spacing folded) duplicate of a train/dev message, also of another dataset's. Fail closed.
* **(b) Content-word signature.** The sorted set of lemmas of proper names, nouns and verbs of the stored `analysis` (`bad_english` has none: a light tokenizer with a stop-word list over the clean target, else the message). The report gives the share of test rows whose set is identical to, or at least 80% contained in, the set of a train/dev row of the same form. Report only.
* **(c) Form signature.** The analysis skeleton of the forms inventory (`eval/reports/current/three-datasets/symbolic-forms-inventory.md`: question type, root part of speech, dependents of the root, subordinate clauses; for `bad_english` the language kind and the source family). The report gives how many test rows share a form with train/dev, the coverage of test forms by train forms, and the test forms with no train/dev counterpart (information; an uncovered form is where a form variant is needed, next section).
* **Lexical duplicate.** Identical content-word signature (at least two words) **and** identical form: a test case that only differs from a train/dev case in a number, a filler or a lead-in. Fail closed, like (a).

`tools/datasets/verify-three-datasets.mjs` runs the fail-closed part and `node check-datasets.mjs` prints the numbers as a report. A sealed file is never edited to satisfy the check: `node tools/datasets/drop-lexical-duplicates.mjs --apply` drops the train/dev rows that duplicate a sealed row (same normalized text, or same content words and form) and updates the manifests; it reads the sealed side only as the hashes of the catalog that the auditor-side `node tools/eval/form-templates.mjs` writes, it is idempotent, and it runs after every rebuild of the three datasets, before the composed and form-variant steps.

### Form coverage and form variants

The only generalization tested is the **same form with different words**; no claim is made about forms the system or a model was not built or trained on. Form coverage is therefore an invariant: every form of a test split has train cases of the same form with different content words, and every form of the training side has a test case. `node tools/datasets/form-variants.mjs` fills gaps from the test side: for a form with fewer than five train/dev rows in `symbolic_english`, deterministic slot substitution on the analysed sentence of a gold-verified sealed row keeps the form and changes the proper names, common nouns and the main verb, with replacements drawn from the train/dev side only (given names, surnames and places of the diversity generator; nouns, verbs and proper names of train/dev analyses; a verb is replaced only by a verb that train/dev rows render with the same relation shape and inflection). The expected SOP is the template's gold SOP with the substituted strings. A variant is kept only when SymbolicLM analyses it as the same form, converts it, produces exactly the expected SOP (modulo id renumbering), and it is neither a text duplicate nor a lexical duplicate of a sealed row. Rows are tagged `source.corpus: form-variant`, `source.form_id` (a hash of the form); no text or id of an evaluation row is kept. The other direction is the same generator run on the training side: `node tools/eval/test-variants.mjs` delexicalizes gold-verified train/dev rows whose form no sealed test row has, fills the slots with other words, verifies the variants the same way (same form, expected SOP, no text or lexical duplicate of any train, dev or sealed row) and writes them, sealed, to `eval/suites/symbolic_english/test-variants.jsonl` with `manifest-variants.json` (the audit split `test-variants`, view only). The generator of working-data variants never reads a sealed row (AGENTS.md rule 9): the auditor-side tool `node tools/eval/form-templates.mjs` reads the sealed forms and writes `eval/reports/current/three-datasets/form-templates.json`, which holds delexicalized templates (no name, noun or verb of a sealed row) and hashes of the sealed texts. Forms that only `neuro_english` has cannot be varied mechanically (SymbolicLM fails on them, so no expected SOP or verified rewrite exists); their number is reported for a teacher-generation pass, and forms that still lack one side are listed in `eval/reports/current/composed-eval/form-variants-report.json`.

**After every rebuild** of the three datasets (and after `node tools/eval/legacy-resplit.mjs` when the legacy suites are touched) the working-data additions are regenerated in this order, each step deterministic and idempotent: `node tools/eval/form-templates.mjs` (catalog and sealed hashes), `node tools/datasets/drop-lexical-duplicates.mjs --apply`, `node tools/datasets/composed-train.mjs --apply`, `node tools/datasets/form-variants.mjs --apply`, `node tools/eval/test-variants.mjs`, `node tools/eval/composed-suites.mjs`, `node tools/eval/composed-tokens.mjs --url <llama-server of the Gemma 3 270M GGUF>`, then `node tools/datasets/verify-three-datasets.mjs`. Rows of `source.corpus` `composed`, `form-variant` and `production` are working data: they are replaced, never hand-edited. Their membership is re-derived by the same analysis gate (`tools/datasets/three-datasets/place.mjs`): a paragraph or variant whose every sentence passes the gate is a `symbolic_english` row, else a `neuro_english` row (its exact SOP comparison stays as `sop_layer`); a sealed test variant that fails the gate is left out of `test-variants.jsonl`. Each generator records the parses and judge items the gate lacks and refuses `--apply` or the write while a verdict is missing (run the judge task over `datasets_sources/resplit_parse_judge/`, then run it again).

### Length and composed evaluation suites

`node tools/datasets/audit/length-analysis.mjs` reports the distribution of words and sentences per message for every dataset split and the archive corpora (`eval/reports/current/composed-eval/length-analysis.md`). `symbolic_english` holds almost only one- and two-sentence messages, so long inputs, several sentences and mixtures of handled and unhandled forms are evaluated by **composed suites**, built by `node tools/eval/composed-suites.mjs` (seeded, deterministic, reading the sealed test rows only) next to the sealed tests: `eval/suites/<dataset>/test-composed.jsonl` with `manifest-composed.json` (hashes of the file and of the source test file, seed, counts, component rejections). The audit page shows them as the extra split `test-composed` (view only, sealed by location).

A **component** is a self-contained message: one sentence, one request or statement, no third-person pronoun or demonstrative that refers outside it, no leading discourse marker or ellipsis word, a verified analysis and, where available, a gold SOP (`tools/eval/composed/components.mjs`). Every case stores its components (role `symbolic`, `neuro` or `bad`, source id, expected text, expected SOP, position), so a failure is attributable. Kinds:

* **K1 all symbolic** (`symbolic_english`): 2, 3, 5 and 8 sentences of different forms. Expected: every sentence is analysed as when alone, and the SOP equals the concatenation of the component SOPs modulo id renumbering and the statements-before-queries order SymbolicLM emits.
* **K2 mixed** (`neuro_english`): symbolic sentences with neuro sentences that have a verified rewrite target (2/1, 3/1, 4/1, 4/2, 5/1, 6/2, 6/3, 8/2, 8/4), at first, middle, last or spread positions. Expected output of SymbolicProofingLLM: neuro sentences replaced by their targets, symbolic sentences unchanged; then SymbolicLM on the result yields the concatenated expected SOPs.
* **K3 identity long** (`symbolic_english`): the longest natural sentences and 12 to 48 sentence all-symbolic paragraphs; the proofing model must not touch them. `tools/eval/composed-tokens.mjs` writes a sidecar with the Gemma 3 270M token length of every case and marks those beyond the training p99 and maximum (`datasets_archive/proofing/proofreader/train.jsonl`: total length p50 37, p99 87, maximum 547).
* **K4 bad mixed** (`bad_english`): clean sentences among Romanian, mixed or noisy sentences with a clean target; expected: clean ones untouched, bad ones turned into their targets.
* **K5 reference across sentences** (`symbolic_english`): a later sentence refers by pronoun to the subject of an earlier one (the name of a symbolic sentence replaced mechanically by the matching pronoun, only where the antecedent is the sole person name of its sentence); the expected SOP has the name. The proofing models never resolve references: SymbolicLM resolves them when it turns the analysis into SOP, so K5 scores SymbolicLM, and the per-sentence pipeline (rewriter on the failing sentence only, pronouns preserved, then SymbolicLM on the reassembled paragraph).
* **K6 decomposition** (`neuro_english`, `bad_english`): test rows of a decomposition type with a verified multi-sentence target (below).

`node tools/eval/composed-score.mjs` scores (i) SymbolicLM alone on K1, K3 and K5 and (ii) any text-to-text rewriter (an endpoint or a command) on K2, K3, K4, K5 and K6, in **two modes**: whole paragraph, and per sentence (the host splits, a gate keeps the sentences that are fine, the rewriter gets only the others one at a time, the host reassembles). The per-sentence mode is the intended design for both proofing models; the whole-paragraph mode is kept as a comparison. The metrics are defined in [DS016](specsLoader.html?spec=DS016-evaluation-metrics.md) "Composed metrics". The evaluation is preregistered (`status/preregistrations/eval-composed-v1.json`); the existing fine-tuned Gemma 3 270M proofreader is measured only as a baseline, nothing is trained.

### Composed training cases

The working data holds long cases of the composed kinds so the proofing models see paragraphs at training time (`node tools/datasets/composed-train.mjs`, run after every rebuild; the rebuild itself is unchanged). They are built from train rows (train cases) and dev rows (dev cases) only, never from the sealed test, so the composed suites and these cases never share a component: `symbolic_english` paragraphs of 2 to 8 known-good sentences whose SymbolicLM analysis equals the concatenation of the parts (checked when built; the identity target of SymbolicProofingLLM is the text itself), and `neuro_english` paragraphs that mix symbolic sentences (unchanged) with neuro sentences that have a verified target (replaced), part of them with decomposition components. Rows carry `composed: true`, `composed_kind`, their components and `source.corpus: composed`. `bad_english` gets no composed paragraphs: both proofing models work one sentence at a time, keep pronouns and references as they are, and never need the previous sentence (owner decision 2026-09-30); a context-dependent case is counted, not trained.

### Decomposition coverage and the target style

The hardest and most important job of both proofing models is **decomposition**: turning a tangled sentence with several clauses into short simple sentences SymbolicLM understands. The target-style contract is [DS021](specsLoader.html?spec=DS021-model-surface.md) "Limited English for SymbolicLM". A **decomposition case** is a row whose message has more than one finite clause (complement clauses of a frame such as "Do you know whether" are not counted) or several questions, and whose verified target has more sentences than the message. `node tools/datasets/audit/decomposition-coverage.mjs [--check-targets]` counts, per dataset and split, the candidates, the candidates with a verified target and the decomposition cases by type (list, multiple questions, coordination, subordinate clause, relative clause; unpunctuated run-ons are counted as a flag), and with `--check-targets` measures how well existing targets follow the contract (clauses per target sentence, explicit subject) from the SymbolicLM analysis of each target sentence. The K6 suite scores a rewriter on these cases: output sentence count against the expected count, every sentence within the contract, SymbolicLM handling every output sentence, names, numbers, negations and connectives preserved, SOP equal to the gold where one exists; a "no rewrite" row shows how SymbolicLM does on the tangled original. The count of decomposition cases with a verified target is small; a teacher-generation pass (an LLM writing targets, checked by the same gates) would be needed to add more, and is not done here.

**Decomposition evaluation set (2026-10-01, `eval/suites/decomposition/`).** K6 has 16 cases; the larger measure of decomposition is `eval/suites/decomposition/test.jsonl` (275 cases, experiment `eval-decomposition-v1`, [DS016](specsLoader.html?spec=DS016-evaluation-metrics.md) "Decomposition and detectability"): tangled clean-English rows of the sealed `neuro_english` test (more than one finite clause by the stored analysis, typed coordination, subordinate, relative, completive, multi_question, run_on, list) and DeepSeek tangled paraphrases of groups of sealed `symbolic_english` TEST sentences for the rare types. The expected decomposition of each case is short sentences with explicit subjects and kept connectives, certified by SymbolicLM and accepted by the two-vote meaning judge (Grok, GLM); the set is sealed, so no builder reads it and the sealed-overlap checker `tools/eval/decomposition/overlap-check.mjs` keeps training data from repeating a case.

### How we learn from production

The system is regression-tested, not proven: in production we learn what goes wrong and add cases. The loop is declarative and has three steps, none of which trains or edits a sealed file.

1. **Add.** `node tools/datasets/add-case.mjs add --message TEXT [--clean TEXT | --rewrite TEXT | --sop TEXT]` classifies the message the way the builders classify it, on the analysis layer: the clean-English gate, then SymbolicLM's analysis and the analysis gate of `analysis-gate.mjs` (identical default and accurate trees; DeepSeek judge conditions a and c). Every sentence passing means `symbolic_english`, a failing sentence `neuro_english`, a message without an analysed sentence or with an `unparsed` span follows the SOP rules like the builders. When a default-package parse or a judge verdict is still missing the case is `pending`, never guessed: it waits in the local queue `datasets_sources/incoming_pending/incoming.jsonl`, `add` records the missing default parse and appends the judge items to `datasets_sources/resplit_parse_judge`, and `add-case.mjs resolve` places the case in its dataset after the omp judge task ran. The row is appended to `datasets/<bad_english|symbolic_english|neuro_english>/incoming.jsonl` with `source.corpus: production`, provenance (who, when, the classifier), the classification verdict, `analysis_verdict`, `sop_layer` and `review_status: pending`. `--clean` is the clean target of a `bad_english` case, `--rewrite` the rewrite target of a `neuro_english` case, `--sop` an optional gold SOP of a `symbolic_english` case (it only fills `sop_layer`; it no longer decides the dataset).
2. **Review.** The owner reviews incoming rows in the audit page (split `incoming`) or with `add-case.mjs review --id ID --status accepted|rejected`. Nothing pending or rejected is ever merged.
3. **Merge.** `add-case.mjs merge --dataset D --into train|dev` moves the accepted rows into the dataset's train or dev file (updating the manifest), refusing a row whose text duplicates a sealed test message, a `symbolic_english` row whose analysis gate did not pass (`analysis_verified` neither `analysis_gate` nor `sop_rule`), and any other row without a target; the incoming row becomes `merged`. A new regression case enters a sealed test file only through the owner's builders (`datasets_sources/new_cases`), never here. `add-case.mjs resolve` places the `pending` cases once their verdicts exist; `build-three-datasets.mjs refresh --texts FILE` re-analyses cached texts whose tree changed, so the next `assemble` reads the current tree. Merged `symbolic_english` rows are part of the regression net at once (`tools/symbolic-regression.mjs` reads train, dev and test).

**Harvest.** `bad_english` is validated end to end through the chain, and the chain's failures feed `neuro_english`: `node tools/datasets/harvest.mjs` runs `bad_english` rows through a LanguageProofingLLM (an endpoint, a command or the shipped `textToCleanEnglish`), per sentence and only on non-clean sentences, and classifies each output: (a) not clean English or content lost (names, numbers, negations; disagreement with a verified target is recorded and counted apart) is a LanguageProofingLLM failure recorded against the row; (b) clean English that SymbolicLM analyses as a known form is a pass; (c) clean English SymbolicLM does not handle is a new `neuro_english` candidate, appended to `datasets/neuro_english/incoming.jsonl` with the source row id, the LLM and its form signature. The report lists the forms the chain produced that neither `symbolic_english` nor `neuro_english` train/dev covers: the forms to add before the next fine-tuning. A run with a model that is not the intended fine-tuned LanguageProofingLLM is a smoke test of the tool.

### Evaluation and evidence

Freeze dataset and approved library before holdout. Compare predicted and gold answers, proof and completeness, abstention, unauthorized persistence, ambiguity, temporal correctness, and latency; retain errors and the actual solver/memory route. Separate memory-only comparisons with fixed SOP and reasoning, reasoning-only comparisons with fixed evidence, and model-end-to-end evaluations with equal knowledge and tools. Report counts, denominators, family/language/source slices, exclusions, external backend skips, provenance, versioned manifests, and uncertainty; do not call fallback an executed solver or report synthetic seed scores as neural performance. `eval/reports/history/` retains historical results and `eval/reports/current/` carries newly observed reports, each tied to the run that produced it.

`eval/run.mjs --file <suite.jsonl> --predictions <predictions.jsonl> --out <report.json>` compares a full set of explicit `{id,sop}` predictions with the gold circuits, or `--config <runtime.json>` selects an explicitly configured formalizer endpoint. The evaluator can report parse/runtime validity, canonical and execution agreement, UNKNOWN, paraphrase and contrast metrics, per-family/language slices, and stage timings; a configured endpoint does not certify model identity or independent human review, and execution agreement over finite worlds does not establish universal program equivalence. Verbalizer number guards have a separate non-certifying evaluation route at `eval/verbalizer.mjs`; those results cannot be conflated with formalizer accuracy.

### Target faithfulness, template leakage and the sealed-test boundary

A grounded model wire states what the message says. For every wire of a grounded type (by default `stated`) and every problem wire (`query`, `constraint`), each entity or quoted constant must be mentioned in the message, and each negation (`not`, or a lexically negative predicate) must be supported by a negation or omission cue in the message; a query takes its cue from the question itself. An unsupported entity or polarity is a target defect to be fixed in the generator, never relabelled. Assumption wires, which carry explicitly marked model-injected assumptions, are counted and reported but exempt from these faithfulness requirements; which wire types are grounded and which are assumptions is audit configuration ([DS020](specsLoader.html?spec=DS020-corpus-audit-tool.md)).

Evaluation validity also depends on the sealed test not being answerable by template recall. Beyond exact-message overlap, template-level leakage between train/dev and the sealed test is an evaluation-validity metric: the share of sealed-test rows whose masked input template, near-duplicate message, entity label head or target skeleton also occurs in train/dev. A result on a sealed test is reported together with its template-leakage measurement; high template overlap means the score measures template recall rather than language understanding. Current values are observations in `eval/reports/current/corpus-audit/<corpus>.json`, not properties recorded here.

The authoritative **sealed evaluation** exports live under `eval/suites/`. No `test.jsonl` is kept under `datasets/` (the registry audit reports any as `dataset_local_test_paths`); `datasets/splits/` may contain only manifests/checksums. Sealed suite exports are read-only evaluation inputs: generators and checkpoint selection must not read their answers. The executable source guard inspects generator reads/imports and training/selection code: `training/cli.mjs` uses `['train','dev']` for train, preflight and token audit; Python training/audit use train/dev and semantic checkpoint selection evaluates `formalizer/dev.jsonl`. The audit records the observed split lists and local corpus paths; static source inspection does not prove semantic non-overlap or guard against arbitrary computed/external IO.

The rule that generators and training/selection code read only train/dev splits has one bounded exception: **sealed auditors**. The corpus audit tools (`tools/datasets/audit-corpus.mjs` and `tools/datasets/audit/**`) may read `eval/suites/<corpus>/test.jsonl` to measure leakage, as the verifier `tools/datasets/verify-corpus.mjs` may to re-execute sealed rows. Their outputs are reports, never generation, training or selection input. Generators and training/selection code must not import a sealed auditor; `eval/leakage.mjs` lists the auditors in `SEALED_AUDITORS`, excludes them from the generator set, and fails the source-boundary audit on any such import.

### Evaluation registry and the non-model baseline

Predictions are explicit artifacts: the evaluator never calls a generator to fill gaps, and a configured endpoint does not certify which model answered. `eval/registry/manifest.json` declares the real-model dev/selection and sealed-test matrix; `eval/registry/baseline.json` declares a separate **non-model** gold-copy sanity matrix. `tools/eval/registry.mjs` audits before evaluation, executes all dev cells before sealed test cells, and refuses to index an incomplete matrix; it reuses `eval/run.mjs` metrics and `eval/contracts.mjs` fractions/distributions and defines no metrics of its own. Run `node tools/eval/registry.mjs audit` before `node tools/eval/registry.mjs run`; the audit writes `eval/reports/current/registry/leakage.json` even when blocked, and a blocked audit exits nonzero. Supply one `{id,sop}` prediction per suite row under the paths declared in the manifest, then rerun the audit. The ordinary registry never fills in missing model predictions with gold answers. `node tools/eval/registry.mjs index` checks every declared cell and its input/report fingerprints; absent predictions, invalid reports, or missing cells stop final indexing. Independent experiments use separate report directories.

The **only observed registry evaluation is the explicitly labeled non-model baseline and gold-copy evaluator sanity check**, not model accuracy. `tools/eval/baseline.mjs` deliberately copies each suite's target into prediction artifacts; this evaluator-only sanity command reads sealed test gold and must never be used for generator, training or checkpoint selection. It verifies identical existing artifacts without rewriting them, and refuses conflicting files. Reproduce the recorded baseline with:

```sh
node tools/eval/baseline.mjs --file datasets_archive/formalizer-v1/dev.jsonl --out eval/predictions/baseline-formalizer-dev-formalization.jsonl
node tools/eval/baseline.mjs --file eval/suites/formalizer-v1/test.jsonl --out eval/predictions/baseline-formalizer-test-formalization.jsonl
node tools/eval/registry.mjs audit eval/registry/baseline.json eval/reports/history/registry-baseline
node tools/eval/registry.mjs run eval/registry/baseline.json eval/reports/history/registry-baseline
node tools/eval/registry.mjs index eval/registry/baseline.json eval/reports/history/registry-baseline
```

Each baseline prediction file has a sidecar manifest recording `predictor_identity: deterministic-baseline-gold-copy`, the exact command and input suite SHA-256; `eval/reports/history/registry-baseline/leakage.json` records the preflight boundary, while `eval/reports/history/registry-baseline/index.json` fingerprints the completed baseline matrix. A real model has supplied neither dev/selection nor sealed-test predictions. The ordinary `eval/registry/manifest.json` audit remains blocked on those two missing cells, demonstrating that a model report cannot silently omit either one.

### Shared semantic cases and adjudication

`tools/datasets/schema.mjs` accepts sourced semantic cases with immutable source ID/kind/URI/revision/SHA-256/license, a `generation_trace`, and `quality_flags`. A raw source excerpt can be supplied as `source.content`, in which case its digest is checked; a remote/raw corpus hash without embedded bytes remains provenance to be verified by the importer and must **not** be mistaken for a hash of `context_assertions`. Rows require `input_mode` (`query_only`, `assertions_query`, `clarification`). Attached assertions are part of the message in `question` (the legacy `context_assertions` field is not model input); `setup_sop` is separately approved background, and may be empty.

Each semantic case has one declarative export target but several distinct surfaces (English, Romanian, mixed, with typing noise); duplicated literal surfaces are rejected, linked negatives share a split, and a negative may retain the same generic epistemic status while changing answers, bindings, outputs, or side effects. Model-origin targets contain only `stated`, `assumed`, `unclear`, `query`, and `constraint` (DS021), ending in a query, a constraint, statements alone, or a lone `unclear`. A `stated` or `assumed` proposition carries strings, no `source`, `quote`, or implicit write. Expected results include four-valued query statuses and supported constraint statuses; `expected.answers` permits typed numeric/structured tuples and `expected.outputs` permits finite JSON values. Host-generated `clarify` results require a real underspecified identity or missing/nonunique required scalar, never an ungrounded model-emitted clarification string. Explicit sourced `fact`/`remember` programs, session `stored` results, and operations such as `expand` belong to a separately named trusted-system evaluation track, never formalizer targets. Distinguish reference/setup failures from candidate failures rather than calling a bad reference a model error.

The model track independently verifies that no statement or assumption becomes a repository claim, while retaining original user text separately from sourced facts. In the trusted-system evaluation track, `expected.session_claims` checks explicitly written atoms, validity intervals, source, quotation and retention independently of a gold circuit's effects. The runtime rejects result-looking literal values at `cnl`; host-expanded answer agreement must come from admitted reasoning, not fabricated packet fields.

Corpus identity compares a **conservative parser-only alpha key**, not raw SOP text: local wire renames and harmless atom spacing do not split one case, but external `~approved` handles remain exact. A genuine structural difference within one case needs explicit semantic adjudication and a selected canonical export target; it is not silently treated as equal or marked wrong solely by text inequality. A negative with only syntactic variation is not a contrast; if its structure is the same, an independently different source/problem world and oracle must substantiate the distinction. Duplicate-case checks include source, approved setup, input mode, timestamp, assertions and expected oracle so identical query text in different worlds is legitimate.

For the model-language corpora, `node tools/datasets/verify-corpus.mjs --corpus formalizer-v1 --all` verifies the manifest checksums (including the sealed, physically disjoint test) and re-executes every row on its verification world; `eval/suites/formalizer-v1/manifest.json` is the sealed suite's checksum authority. Validating a checksum or passing the independent finite/graph oracle is **not** source-rights clearance, human semantic signoff, or training authorization. No optimizer may be run until a new explicit user approval.

`tools/datasets/semantic-compare.mjs` conservatively normalizes harmless definition-name/variable changes and compares through **the same guarded evaluator** used for model evaluations, including supplied discriminating worlds. A mismatched execution signature is a counterexample even if both status labels match. Passing finitely many worlds without safe alpha identity remains `pending`, not universally proven equivalent. `skills/semantic-sop-review/SKILL.md` describes immutable bundle creation, an actual independent LLM review receipt with true model/prompt/context/circuit/probe hashes and specific rationale for unresolved differences, and a separate principal-integrator decision. An unavailable reviewer yields quarantine; neither an LLM nor a principal can override failed syntax, types, policy, reference setup, or an observed counterexample.
