Overview / Input language

Input language: from what the user types to what SymbolicLM understands

Users do not type clean English. They write Romanian, English, a mixture of both ("romgleza") and carelessly spelled text, while SymbolicLM analyses only clean, simple English reliably. This page describes the chain of host components that bridges the two, the heuristics each one applies, what each small model can and cannot learn, how the chain is judged, and what is still open. It describes design and current state; it does not assert progress. Owner direction of 2026-10-01: do what can be done, document it, add heuristics, show the user in the chat what the system understood, and tune over time from that feedback (status/journal.jsonl, events of 2026-10-01).

Status labels used below: implemented (code exists and is exercised), in progress (an agent is working on it; not yet in the product path), planned (decided or proposed, no code). The model boundary of DS021 is unchanged: every step on this page is host work before the formalizer, and the formalizer's input remains the text the user finally accepted.

1. The chain

  1. User messageRomanian, English, mixed or badly written; one or several sentences.
  2. LanguagesUtilLanguage per token, frame (matrix) language of the sentence, conservative symbolic spelling. lib/languages-util/
  3. TranslatorServiceSymbolic translation toward English with the reviewed dictionary; a morphology-aware word-by-word gloss mode is in progress. lib/translator-service/
  4. LanguageProofingLLM (textToCleanEnglish)Turns bad input into acceptable English, sentence by sentence, every sentence sent (llm.sendAll), and the user validates the proposal (Accept, Edit, Send original). lib/text-to-clean-english/
  5. SymbolicProofingLLMRewrites correct English that SymbolicLM cannot certify into the limited English it understands; optional, off unless wired.
  6. SymbolicLMStanza accurate analysis plus the UD-to-SOP rules; reports an uncertainty signal and the grammatical analysis. lib/symbolic-lm/
  7. Interpretation shown back to the user"I understood: ...", so the user can rephrase when it is wrong (in progress, section 4).

Ownership of each component, its inputs and its outputs is in DS021 (sections "LanguagesUtil", "TranslatorService", "textToCleanEnglish", "SymbolicLM", "Limited English for SymbolicLM"); the chat integration is in DS012, and Runtime describes the user-validated step. The two proofing models are defined in the wiki. In the current product path the symbolic translator is the only TranslatorService backend wired into SymbolicLM; the cleaning step and the SymbolicProofingLLM rewrite are separate, optional host steps with their own switches (config/text-to-clean-english.json, config/formalizers.json).

2. Romanian and mixed Romanian/English ("romgleza") heuristics

Heuristics are symbolic, inspectable and deliberately conservative: a word that no rule or dictionary entry covers is kept and marked, never guessed. Each is cheap enough to run before any model.

HeuristicStatusWhat exists and where
Per-token language identificationimplementedEach token is labelled Romanian, English, name, number, punctuation or other, with its evidence (closed-class words, diacritics, suffix morphology such as Romanian -ul, -ilor, -esc versus English -ing, -ed, -tion, hyphenated clitic clusters such as i-a, capitalization); the message is en, ro or mixed. lib/languages-util/langid.mjs.

Not yet merged into the product path.
English nouns with Romanian articles or plurals ("deadline-ul", "task-urile")implemented (symbolic backend); gloss mode in progressThe symbolic translator treats a hyphenated -ul, -ului, -urile, -ii, -le after a foreign word as the Romanian definite article, so "gateway-ul" becomes "the gateway", and maps Romanian plural endings after an English stem ("mailuri" to "mails"). lib/translator-service/backends/symbolic.mjs.
English verbs with Romanian inflection ("am forwardat", "share-uiesc")plannedLanguage identification already notes Romanian verb morphology (-esc, -ează, -ând) as evidence, but no rule yet strips a Romanian ending from an English stem and re-inflects it in English. It belongs to the gloss mode. Until then such sentences rely on LanguageProofingLLM and on the uncertainty signal.
English-frame sentences with inserted Romanian words ("check the factura")in progressFrame detection identifies the English frame; the inserted word is glossed only if the reviewed dictionary knows it, otherwise it is reported as untranslated. LanguageProofingLLM covers the mixed class of bad_english but is not reliable on unseen Romanian words (section 3).
Unknown words copied and marked, never guessedimplementedA content word that the dictionary does not know is copied verbatim and reported as untranslated; the uncertainty signal lists it so the chain can escalate or ask. lib/translator-service/backends/symbolic.mjs, lib/symbolic-lm/uncertainty.mjs; contract in DS021.
Dropped Romanian subjects become pronouns; cross-sentence references are resolved by SymbolicLMimplemented (narrow)The translator inserts a pronoun for a pro-drop subject and marks it (pro-drop); SymbolicLM leaves such a role open rather than inventing a referent, and the UD-to-SOP rules resolve a third-person pronoun to a person named earlier in the message and report an ambiguous reading when two parallel clauses compete. Wider coreference is not claimed. lib/symbolic-lm/index.mjs, lib/ud-to-sop/analyze.mjs.

Measured limits of the older approach: manual reading of 50 Opus-MT outputs for mixed text found 21 of 50 fully faithful, which is why a neural machine translator is not the default (DS021, "TranslatorService", owner question 1; experiment eval-translator-compare-v1).

3. What the small models do and do not do

LanguageProofingLLM: grammar and structure, not unseen vocabulary

The fine-tuned Gemma 3 270M learns the task shape (Romanian, mixed and noisy English to acceptable English) but does not generalize to words it never saw as targets. Vocabulary therefore comes from dictionaries and generated coverage data, not from the weights.

  • Held-out vocabulary. On 574 pairs whose target contains a word absent from training, iteration 2 keeps the held-out word in 45.8% of outputs (iteration 1 49.1%, untrained base 36.4%, no rewrite 42.1%); Romanian inputs with an unseen word score about 20% for every arm. Report: eval/reports/current/language-proofing-it2/summary.md.
  • The child to parent incident. Iteration 1 trained on targets with "parent of" 249 times and "child of" never, and turned Romanian copilul lui and English "child of" into "parent" (204 of 229 probe units, 89.1%), reversing a family relation. The meaning judge found 18.6% of non-English outputs not meaning-preserving. Report: eval/reports/current/language-proofing-it1/summary.md.
  • The fix. Iteration 2 added vocabulary-covering back-generated data and a held-out-vocabulary dev set: the relation word is kept in 229 of 229 probe units and the flip occurs once; clean sentences stay untouched in 98.1% (iteration 1: 94.8%); keyboard mash is returned unchanged in 98.7% (66.7%). The composite score is unchanged (66.3% against 66.1%) and per-sentence repair of bad sentences is lower; the chat registry was switched to iteration 2 under the owner rule that catastrophic interpretations must be about zero (journal, 2026-10-01T03:27). Report: eval/reports/current/language-proofing-it2/summary.md.

SymbolicProofingLLM: cannot see the parser's boundary

The small rewriter does not know which sentences SymbolicLM already analyses correctly and which it cannot, so it also rewrites sentences that were fine. In iteration 2 it repaired 37.6% of the sealed pairs (iteration 1: 9.3%) but changed 19.8% of sentences that needed no change (sealed identity; eval/reports/current/symbolic-proofing-it2/; journal, 2026-10-01T03:01). Two host measures follow:

  • A local routing gate and an acceptance check around the rewrite (in progress, preregistered eval-symbolic-pipeline-gate-v1): send a sentence to the rewriter only when the default and accurate parse trees differ or SymbolicLM is uncertain, and accept the output only if SymbolicLM can certify it, mechanical meaning checks pass and the analysis comparison does not say "different". Work area: eval/reports/current/symbolic-pipeline-gate/.
  • Decomposition into short sentences with explicit subjects (implemented as training pairs, effect measured partly): iteration 2 reaches the correct sentence count in 32.8% of sealed decomposition cases (iteration 1: 4.5%). Pronouns are kept so that SymbolicLM, not the rewriter, resolves cross-sentence references.

4. "Good enough, never catastrophic, never nothing"

Owner direction of 2026-10-01: the first phase is a good-enough system with clear room for improvement. Large language models also lose nuances and users rephrase. What matters is that no interpretation is catastrophic (opposite meaning, swapped roles, wrong entity, flipped negation) and that the system never answers "no interpretation" when a clear reading exists.

  • Graded severity (in progress, severity-eval-agent): S0 equivalent, S1 nuance, S2 partial, S3 wrong but noticeable, S4 catastrophic, and NONE, reported next to pass/fail. The grader is a cascade: mechanical checks, analysis comparison, then an LLM severity judge. On the 702 calibration items the local layers alone never call a non-S4 item S4 (0 of 455) but decide only 218 items; GLM-5.3-flash as severity judge reaches 85.0% exact and 93.1% S4 recall, Grok 81.2% and 91.5%. Report: eval/reports/current/severity/calibration.md; contract: DS016, "Graded severity".
  • The fallback cascade (in progress; design stated by the owner, outputs under eval/reports/current/severity/fallback/): rewrite; then sampled rewrites; then the alternative paraphraser; then symbolic simplification; then a clarification question. Every stage is checked by the acceptance rules above, and the last stage guarantees that the user gets some interpretation or a specific question.
  • The interpretation CNL (in progress, analysis-cnl-agent): a deterministic realizer from SymbolicLM's analysis to a small controlled English ("I understood: ...") with a round-trip check through SymbolicLM and an explicit list of spans it does not represent. On 300 sealed symbolic_english rows, 98.6% round-trip and 87.3% are fully verified; on 200 neuro_english rows 85.6% round-trip (experiment eval-analysis-cnl-v1; eval/reports/current/analysis-cnl/summary.json). It is wired into the chat (implemented, 2026-10-01): under each Formalize answer an "I understood:" panel shows, per sentence, the CNL, an "uncertain interpretation" marker with a raw analysis summary when the round trip fails, the spans not represented (highlighted in the message), whether the analysis is certified, and the SymbolicProofingLLM rewrite trace (original, rewrite, accepted or rejected). Three settings, remembered per browser, control it (clean text, send every sentence, rewrite off/gated/always, show the panel); the chat API returns the same data in chatSop.understanding (DS012 "Understanding in the chat"). The user sees what was understood and rephrases when it is wrong; the owner judges it by manual testing.
  • The production feedback loop (implemented as tools, planned as a chat button): tools/datasets/add-case.mjs adds one reviewed case, and tools/datasets/harvest.mjs turns collected chat feedback into new cases for bad_english, neuro_english or symbolic_english (smoke-tested only; journal, 2026-09-30T18:03). New cases enter train/dev; the sealed tests stay untouched (DS008).

5. How we evaluate

  • Layer by layer. Owner decision of 2026-09-30: for now the proofing models are judged on natural language and grammatical analysis (is the analysis correct, is the meaning kept, is a working sentence left untouched). Translation of the analysis into SOP Lang is a later layer, and SOP metrics are secondary until both proofing models are stable (DS016, "SymbolicProofingLLM evaluation: analysis layer").
  • The same form with different words. Composed and form-variant suites keep the sentence shape and change the vocabulary, and held-out-vocabulary dev sets hold out lexical items, so a model that memorized words is distinguished from one that learned structure (eval/reports/current/composed-eval/summary.md, eval/reports/current/language-proofing-it2/summary.md).
  • Local-first checks. Mechanical checks (names, numbers, negation, question marks, clean-English gate), then the analysis comparison, run before any LLM judge; the judge sees only the residue. The analysis comparison alone was not adopted as a judge (mechanical recall 98.7%, false alarm 11.6% against a 10% rule), only as a cost filter (DS016, "Analysis comparison").
  • Calibrated LLM judges and the routing rule. Each judge is calibrated on a labelled set before use. Owner rule of 2026-10-01: route by calibrated competence; use the z.ai (GLM) and Grok subscriptions first where they calibrate well and switch as soon as one errors or runs out; then local models where competitive; then DeepSeek; Haiku last.

6. Known gaps and how we tune over time

  • Romanian or mixed words outside the dictionary. They are copied and marked, and LanguageProofingLLM does not learn them from fine-tuning. Tuning: grow the reviewed dictionary and the vocabulary-covering training data from the words users actually type.
  • English verbs with Romanian inflection have no dedicated rule yet (section 2). Tuning: add them to the gloss mode with tests from harvested cases.
  • Frame detection does not yet steer the default pipeline. Tuning: use it to choose between gloss, cleaning and no change once the gloss mode is merged.
  • SymbolicProofingLLM still changes sentences that were correct (19.8% sealed identity change in iteration 2). Tuning: the routing gate and acceptance check, then more decomposition and identity data.
  • The severity grader over-calls S4 for some S3 items (false S4 8.1% to 14.9% depending on the arm). Tuning: more hand-assigned hard cases and a stricter two-judge rule.
  • The interpretation CNL leaves spans unrepresented on 11% (symbolic_english) to 28% (neuro_english) of sampled rows and the chat now shows those spans with the interpretation. Tuning: extend templates from the not-represented lists.
  • Wider coreference beyond named antecedents and pro-drop subjects is not attempted.
  • Feedback. The chat does not yet collect "this is not what I meant" automatically; add-case and harvest are manual tools. Tuning: each reviewed correction becomes a case, the regression suite (tools/symbolic-regression.mjs) guards against regressions, and open owner decisions go to questions.md.

Open work is tracked in TODO.md; delivered work in PAS_TASK.md; the project timeline is at /experiments.

7. Tone, courtesy and the parts SymbolicLM cannot formalize

Greetings, thanks, politeness, urgency, hedges, frustration, swearing, discourse markers and tags such as "right?" carry no relation SymbolicLM can formalize, but they say how the message should be answered. The EmotionDetectionSystem (DS029) is a separate component that can be switched off (chat setting "Detect tone and courtesy", enabled in config/emotion-detection.json). After SymbolicLM it classifies the whole message and every span the analysis does not represent by its pragmatic or emotional role, and the "I understood" panel lists those spans with their kind instead of leaving them as "not represented". A span that no strategy can name stays unparsed.

  • Strategies. A symbolic one (English and Romanian lexicons and patterns, emoji, punctuation, capitals; 0.2 ms) is on by default. Optional neural classifiers read the clean English text (35 to 190 ms on a laptop CPU) and an LLM slot is empty; both are off because the evaluation did not show a useful gain (summary: eval/reports/current/emotion-detection/summary.md).
  • What the reasoner may do. The signals are emitted as advisory pragmatic wires and are never facts about the world: urgency asks for a shorter answer and a faster strategy; frustration or confusion re-checks the previous interpretation or offers a clarification; a hedge keeps the statement a supposition; a message that only greets or thanks gets a short courtesy reply without computation; a possible irony is never acted on literally without confirmation.
  • Honest limits. On a 300-message development set the symbolic strategy reaches about 96% precision and 88% recall against two LLM judges (93% / 81% on the half that was not used to correct the lexicon); irony and joy are weak and kept soft or experimental; Romanian is covered by the lexicon only, since the neural models read English.

8. The stages as independent APIs

Each stage of the chain in section 1 has its own endpoint, so another project can use any of them alone and the chat page calls them one by one: POST /v1/language/proofread (LanguageProofingLLM, the proposal the user validates), POST /v1/understand (SymbolicLM: the "I understood" interpretation with certification, not-represented spans, leftovers classified by tone, a clarify message), POST /v1/symbolic/rewrite (SymbolicProofingLLM), POST /v1/symbolic/analyze (the raw analysis) and POST /v1/emotion/detect (signals and emoticons). implemented

  • Never a total failure. If part of a message cannot be analysed, the answer still carries what was understood; the failed sentence, the unrepresented span or the uncertain sentence is marked, errors says which component failed, and clarify proposes a follow-up such as I did not understand: “…”. Could you rephrase it?.
  • Caches. A small cache with in-flight de-duplication sits in front of every model call and the SymbolicLM analysis, keyed by capability, model run, exact input and options, so the final answer reuses what the page asked for while the user was reading the proposal.
